# Chapter 3 — critic and editor pass

## Pass 1 — improvised, no plugin loaded

## Where both agents agreed

**The chapter needed almost nothing.** This is the strongest draft of the six
on a first pass — no flat-period problem (question marks are used throughout),
no em dash standing in for the ellipsis convention, no exclamation-mark relay
residue on Sable or Aldric's lines, no escalating triads or negation pairs.
The chapter that had been flagged hardest in earlier rounds (5, 6) does not
repeat here. The only real finding was density in one construction, not a
structural or voice problem.

**The reveal is well paced.** Leo laying out eleven months of casework before
Agnes says a word, the two "Alright"s (his to her build-up, hers to something
unheard), and the ghosts' entrance landing right after Leo's hardest line —
all protected, don't touch.

**The ending is already right.** *"Okay."* alone, no tag, is exactly the
still-target the checklist asks for after a chapter that could easily have
closed on one more image of Leo's composure cracking. Left as is.

## The critic's headline finding

**Four "like X" constructions in one continuous scene**, against the
one-per-scene ceiling in the then-current `kb/issues/prose-tics.md`, now archived at `kb/archive/prose-tics-superseded.md`. Individually each was
doing real work — none read as filler on its own — but stacked across a
single unbroken scene they read as a tic by the fourth occurrence, which is
exactly the failure mode the rule exists to catch.

Ranked and resolved:

- *"She took a breath like she was about to lift something heavy"* — kept.
  Physical, earns its place immediately before the hardest thing she has to
  say.
- *"'Sable,' she said finally, one word, like a formality she'd decided he
  was owed"* — kept. Characterizes a four-hundred-year-old ghost's register
  in five words, on his first line in the manuscript.
- *"She said it steady, like an old argument she had stopped trying to win
  fast"* — cut. This one is the exact bolted-on-second-clause shape the
  checklist names: a specific beat ("steady") followed by a "like X" that
  explains rather than adds. "Steady" already carries it.
- *"'They're still here,' Agnes said, like she was continuing a sentence
  instead of starting one"* — cut. A second construction characterizing
  Agnes's speech pattern was one too many once the breath-like-lifting line
  already had that job; the line reads clean without it.

## Applied line work

| Finding | Change |
|---|---|
| Reaction filler ("she was quiet a moment. Then:") ahead of Agnes's line, matching the pattern flagged under "Reaction filler standing in for a beat" | *"When he was done she was quiet a moment. Then:"* → *"When he was done, she said,"* |
| Bolted-on second clause on a dialogue tag | *"She said it steady, like an old argument she had stopped trying to win fast."* → *"She said it steady."* |
| Fourth "like X" construction in one scene | *"Agnes said, like she was continuing a sentence instead of starting one."* → *"Agnes said."* |

## Left alone, deliberately

- The two "Alright"s (Leo's and Agnes's) — read as an intentional echo across
  the confession, not a repeated-word tic; different speakers, different
  addressees, four lines apart in the surrounding text.
- "Eleven months" stated twice (Leo's case-history and his line about being
  disbelieved) — a deliberate callback reinforcing the stakes, not the
  within-sentence word-repeat the checklist warns about.
- "Not solid." and "None of it reached his face." — two short fragment-verdict
  beats. Below the checklist's density threshold (more than two or three
  across a chapter) and each is doing specific work: one describes the
  ghosts' materiality, the other is Leo's on-page restraint. Not a pattern
  yet; watch it in chapter 4 onward.

## Pass 2 — real critic agent, two lanes

Run once the plugin was actually installed, to give the chapter the pass it
hadn't had yet. This surfaced findings pass 1 couldn't have — a canon
discrepancy against the outline, an unpiloted character's voice, and a
redundant-interiority instance pass 1 missed entirely.

### Voice/prose lane

- **Redundant interiority after the apparition.** *"He was, on the inside,
  screaming. None of it reached his face."* labels the emotion and then
  labels its suppression, both already shown by *"Leo's hands, he noticed
  distantly, were flat on the table, pressing down."* This is the manuscript's
  most documented failure pattern (see the archived checklist's "Redundant
  work" section) and pass 1 didn't catch it. **Applied:** cut *"He was, on the
  inside, screaming."*; the flat-hands beat and *"None of it reached his
  face"* carry it alone.
- **Aldric's "kudos for not screaming yet."** Reads as contemporary/meta for
  a 400-year-old cleric with no settled voice notes — effectively setting a
  voice precedent for an unpiloted character, which `CLAUDE.md` says isn't a
  writer's call to make solo. Put to the author: **softened**, not fully
  repiloted. *"Nice to meet you. Genuinely, kudos for not screaming yet."* →
  *"Nice to meet you. Most people scream, first time."* Keeps the warmth and
  the dry tease, drops the internet-register "kudos." Aldric's voice is still
  unsettled and should get the same disposable-pilot-scene treatment Leo's
  and Agnes's got before it's treated as fact.
- **Agnes's doubled ask-for-permission beat** — *"Before I start, I need you
  to let me finish. Can you do that?"* (line 17) and *"I'd rather just say it
  than spend five minutes arguing with you about whether I've lost it"*
  (line 25) do adjacent work. **Considered, left alone**: the intervening
  exchange (*"Depends what it is." "That's not a yes." "It's the best you're
  getting tonight."*) is some of the chapter's best character work and
  depends on line 17's question to function; cutting it costs more than the
  soft redundancy is worth. Watch for it recurring elsewhere rather than
  fixing it here.
- **Post-apparition exposition slightly tidy** — Agnes's three clean
  sentences on what it costs the ghosts to be seen, right after Leo watches
  two dead people appear and vanish. **Considered, left alone**: minor, and
  the content (why they left, why she does the talking) is information Leo
  needs stated plainly.
- Everything else — POV discipline, Leo's flat-declarative-question register,
  Sable's introduction, the surviving "like X" constructions, sentence
  rhythm — checked out clean.

### Structure/continuity lane

- **Direction-of-travel contradiction.** The outline's beat 12 said Geoff was
  headed *to* Mullerfield for the fair; the draft has both Leo and Agnes
  saying he was driving *home from* it. Put to the author: **keep the draft**.
  **Applied:** updated `work/outline/book1-chapters-02-04-beats.md` beat 12 to
  match.
- **Geoff's first name never lands in Leo's pitch**, though Leo's line at 31
  ("How do you know his last name") implies Agnes already had the first name
  from him, and the outline's own beat 10 has Leo naming him during the pitch.
  **Applied:** *"The last one was a kid."* → *"The last one was a kid named
  Geoff."*
- **Chapter heading "Eight Thirty"** flagged against `CLAUDE.md`'s
  don't-title-chapters rule — but chapters 1, 4, and 6 are also titled while
  2 and 5 aren't, an existing author pattern predating this session. **No
  action**; already checked against the other drafts before this pass.
- **The half-second audio/visual desync on Aldric's voice** isn't attested in
  `kb/world/ghost-mechanics.md`. **Left as one-off atmospheric color, not
  added to canon** — flagging here in case it recurs and needs to become a
  real rule rather than an accidental one.
- **The abrupt ending** (six lines of aftermath after the reveal, closing on
  "Okay") — flagged as a structural observation, not a defect: in character
  for Leo's restraint, but worth the author's attention if it reads as too
  clipped on a read-through. **No action**, author's call.
- All six assigned beats (10–15) confirmed delivered in order; ghost
  appearance, perception-tier logic, and character-page facts otherwise
  checked out clean against the kb.
