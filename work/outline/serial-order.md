Cross-references below use the same shorthand as the rest of the kb: bare filenames are relative to `kb/arcs/`.

### Rationale, arc by arc

- **11 (Alegrove).** Needs the full seven-person crew; the emitter doesn't exist yet at this point either way, so it reads as the team solving an impossible case on presence and instinct alone, before the toolkit changes. (Its "no emitter" tag was never about the emitter's existence — see the note on arc 18.)
- **12–14 (Crypt → CT Scans → Emitter).** Strict order already, no alternative — this chain is what invents the emitter.
- **15 (Julian Mission).** First field use of the emitter, and it goes badly. The Emitter arc itself already ends on a joyful first-use montage (bread, held hands, reading) — this lands as the immediate cost right after that high.
- **16 (Hans Fischer/Piano).** A found-family breather after Julian Mission's aftermath, and the arc that puts the Fischer lineage — and Aura's existence — in front of the reader for the first time, unexplained.
- **17 (Aura Chronicle).** Answers what Hans Fischer's arc raised: the full ~420-year backstory of Aura, the pact, Lamby, the fever. Presented here rather than left as kb-only reference, so the Wishing House (next) has the context it needs to land.
- **18 (Wishing House).** Sits after the Aura Chronicle rather than right after Foundation, because Room 4 puts an *alternate* Aura on the page — one who never had to make the pact, unmarked, powerful — and that only lands if the reader already has the real Aura's history to measure it against. This is ordinary chronological order, not a flashback: by this point the emitter exists, but "no emitter" was never about the emitter's existence — it's the crew's call on the day. A missing nine-year-old last seen at a condemned house reads like a routine search, not a job anyone preps solid-ghost gear for, so they go in without it and only discover over the course of the arc how badly they need it. (This reading is now reflected in `arcs/index.md` and the arc's own overview page — see the "emitter is the hinge" note there.)
- **19 (Echo State/Spider).** The largest single arc in the set — echo states, the Crossing, the pocket reality, the revelation about the real ceiling on their power. Positioned as Book 2's climax, right after the Aura/Wishing House pair returns to forward momentum.
- **20 (Vampire Coven).** Direct sequel to Julian Mission via Daniel Vega's throughline; runs this much later because it isn't competing with Echo State/Spider for climax weight, and the arc's own text implies real time has passed for Daniel Vega to become an established operative anyway.
- **21 (Holy Rot/Cookie's Void).** No dependencies, placed here as a standalone gut-punch after the emitter arcs — deliberately echoes Sable's original poisoning in Origin (arc 1), which lands harder read this far in. *(Also movable — nothing forces it here.)*
- **22 (Lich's Wheel).** Self-contained alt-timeline special; closes Book 2 as a bonus episode rather than setting up the next volume.

## Held back — not yet scheduled

- **Horatio Martinez / The Memory-Thief Watchmaker** — ends unresolved as written (Sable choosing to reclaim her memories, then nothing past that point). Needs an ending before it gets a slot; likely reads best as a cliffhanger into Book 3 once it has one.
- **The Smuggling Operation** — still a stub with an undecided parent-discovery branch. Needs the branch decided, and probably fleshed out past its current one-paragraph shape, before it can be scheduled.

## Open questions

- **The Aura Chronicle's presentation (arc 17).** Placed as an interlude per this decision, but *how* it's delivered on the page (a chapter of its own, told secondhand through Aldric, a document Hans's family produces, etc.) is a drafting choice, not a sequencing one — open for whenever that arc gets written.
- **Book 1's chapter-level presentation.** See `book1-opening-chapters.md` — the arc order above stays intact, but the actual chapters open on Leo rather than Origin. Worth checking, once that's drafted, whether the same reader-first logic changes how any other Foundation arc gets presented.
