# The Fifth of June

The woman across the desk had been talking for four minutes and Leo hadn't written anything down.

Her name was Kolbeck. She owned six units on Ferris and she had brought a folder, which he appreciated, and a printout of the lease with three lines highlighted in yellow, which he appreciated less, because it meant she had already decided what this was and had come here to have it agreed with.

She sat the way people sat in an office they expected to be reimbursed for visiting: forward on the seat, weight in her feet, nothing committed to the chair.

"Two months," she said. "Two months and eleven days, if we're counting from the fifth."

"We can count from the fifth."

"He stopped answering in June. I left messages. I'm not an unreasonable woman about this, I gave it a full week before I called at all, people have things going on."

The radiator did in August what it did every August, which was nothing.

"Renter's name."

"Dominic Ruiz. It's on the lease, I highlighted it."

"How old is he."

"It's on the lease."

"I know what's on the lease. How old does he look."

That stopped her, which was interesting, because it was the first question she hadn't already answered in the car on the way over. People came in with a version. You could usually find the edge of it inside four questions, and the edge was always at the place where the version needed her to have not looked at something.

"Thirty-five. Thin. He carries his bicycle up the stairs, which I've told him about."

"When did you last see him. Not spoke to. Saw."

"The fifth, when he..." She stopped again. "The first week of June. The bicycle, the stairs. I told him again and he said he'd stop."

"Did he stop."

"No."

"Where's the bike now."

There was a pause with something in it.

"In the unit."

Leo turned his pen over once and put it down. There it was, and it had taken her under a minute to hand it to him, and she hadn't heard herself do it.

"You've been inside the apartment."

"I'm permitted entry for maintenance with notice."

"I'm not asking whether you're permitted."

"...Yes. Twice."

"Tell me what's in there."

She did, and it was the wrong list. The bicycle against the radiator. A coat on the hook by the door, the heavy one, in June. A pan on the stove with something in it that she described in a way that meant she had looked at it closely and had then chosen different words for it out here. Bed unmade. Toothbrush in a glass.

"Shoes," Leo said.

"What?"

"By the door. How many pairs."

"...Three."

He looked at the lease for a while. Three lines in yellow: the rent, the late fee, the clause about abandonment of premises. Nobody abandoned premises in their socks.

"Two months and eleven days."

"Yes."

"And you called a private investigator."

"I want my money. He owes me eleven hundred dollars and the unit is unrentable while his things are in it, and if I put them on the curb he can come back and say I..."

"Do you know what a welfare check is?"

"I'm not going to be lectured about..."

"It's free," Leo said. "You call, they go, it costs you nothing. You've had eleven hundred dollars' worth of reasons for two months not to make a phone call that's free."

She set her jaw and didn't answer, and he watched her not answer, and there it was in full. A man who ran could be sued. A man who hadn't run couldn't. She had known which one this was since the second time she stood in that apartment, and she had spent the better part of a summer arranging to be told the other thing by somebody she paid to tell her.

Seventy-one days. He kept coming back to it while she talked about the curb and her rights and the unrentable unit. Seventy-one days of a coat on a hook, and a pan on a stove, and a woman going up there twice with her own key and coming back down and doing her accounts.

He should have sent her out. He had a hundred and forty dollars of his own rent to find by the tenth and this paid badly at the front and worse at the back, and he could see both endings from where he sat. In one of them he turned up Dominic Ruiz in a rented room two counties over having the worst year of his life, and Kolbeck got an address for her lawyer, and that was the good one. In the other one he stood on the sidewalk on Ferris with a phone in his hand while men he used to work beside went up the stairs past him.

"Four hundred to start," he said. "Against ten days. I'll want the key."

"The key is for maintenance."

"Mrs. Kolbeck."

"I don't see why you'd need..."

"Give me the key."
