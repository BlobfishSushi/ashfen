# The Fifth of June

The woman across the desk has been talking for four minutes and Leo hasn't written anything down.

Her name is Kolbeck. She owns six units on Ferris and she has brought a folder, which he appreciates, and a printout of the lease with three lines highlighted in yellow, which he appreciates less, because it means she has already decided what this is and has come here to have it agreed with.

She sits the way people sit in an office they expect to be reimbursed for visiting: forward on the seat, weight in her feet, nothing committed to the chair.

"Two months," she says. "Two months and eleven days, if we're counting from the fifth."

"We can count from the fifth."

"He stopped answering in June. I left messages. I'm not an unreasonable woman about this, I gave it a full week before I called at all, people have things going on."

The radiator does what it does in August, which is nothing.

"Renter's name."

"Dominic Ruiz. It's on the lease, I highlighted it."

"How old is he."

"It's on the lease."

"I know what's on the lease. How old does he look."

She stops. It's the first question she hasn't already answered in the car on the way over.

"Thirty-five. Thin. He carries his bicycle up the stairs, which I've told him about."

"When did you last see him. Not spoke to. Saw."

"The fifth, when he..." She stops again. "The first week of June. The bicycle, the stairs. I told him again and he said he'd stop."

"Did he stop."

"No."

"Where's the bike now."

A pause with something in it.

"In the unit."

Leo turns his pen over once and puts it down.

"You've been inside the apartment."

"I'm permitted entry for maintenance with notice."

"I'm not asking whether you're permitted."

"...Yes. Twice."

"Tell me what's in there."

She does, and it is the wrong list. The bicycle against the radiator. A coat on the hook by the door, the heavy one, in June. A pan on the stove with something in it that she describes in a way that means she looked at it closely and then chose different words for it out here. Bed unmade. Toothbrush in a glass.

"Shoes," Leo says.

"What?"

"By the door. How many pairs."

"...Three."

Leo looks at the lease for a while. Three lines in yellow: the rent, the late fee, the clause about abandonment of premises.

"Two months and eleven days."

"Yes."

"And you called a private investigator."

"I want my money. He owes me eleven hundred dollars and the unit is unrentable while his things are in it, and if I put them on the curb he can come back and say I..."

"Do you know what a welfare check is?"

"I'm not going to be lectured about..."

"It's free," Leo says. "You call, they go, it costs you nothing. You've had eleven hundred dollars' worth of reasons for two months not to make a phone call that's free."

She sets her jaw and doesn't answer, and he watches her not answer, and he understands the shape of it: a man who ran can be sued. A man who didn't run can't. She has known which one this was since the second time she stood in that apartment and she has spent two months arranging to be told the other thing by somebody she paid.

He should send her out. He has one hundred and forty dollars of his own rent to find by the tenth and this pays badly at the front and worse at the back, and he knows both endings from here. In one of them he turns up Dominic Ruiz in a rented room two counties over having the worst year of his life, and Kolbeck gets an address for her lawyer, and that's the good one. In the other one he stands on the sidewalk on Ferris with a phone in his hand while people he used to work beside go up the stairs past him.

"Four hundred to start," he says. "Against ten days. I'll want the key."

"The key is for maintenance."

"Mrs. Kolbeck."

"I don't see why you'd need..."

"Give me the key."
