# A Step Closer

Agnes topped off his coffee without asking, which was either hospitality or something to do with her hands. Leo didn't touch it.

She went still again, listening to something on the other side of the room.

"There's a..." She stopped. Started again, flatter. "There's a wraith. Working that stretch of road specifically. Going after people who came through Mullerfield first." A pause, her head tilting, not quite following it herself. "Why that town, I don't know. They don't either. Still out there, as far as they know."

"A wraith."

"That's the word they used."

"Come again."

"That's the word they used," Agnes said, patient about it.

"Great." Leo picked up the coffee and didn't drink it. He turned the mug a quarter turn on the table instead. "And here I was thinking a dead witness was going to be the strangest part of my night."

"The other five," he said. "Same thing?"

Agnes went quiet, listening. "They think so. Nobody else stuck around long enough to say for sure."

"Six wrecks, one shape." He wasn't really asking her anymore.

"Geoff's the one who told them," Agnes went on. "Said he saw it, right before. Knew it wasn't an accident, and he's spent every month since trying to get that to somebody who could do something with it. That's why he's been following you."

Leo's hand found his own collar before he told it to. Months of blaming a bad furnace and a window that didn't seal right, and it had gone away this afternoon, in this room, and he had told himself it was the ovens.

"Was," Agnes said. "Following you."

The past tense got to him half a beat behind the rest of it. "Wait. Is he..."

"Gone. Moved on, a couple hours ago. His business is done, or as close as it gets."

"The corner table." He said it before he'd decided to say anything. Two cold cups of tea he'd clocked that afternoon and filed away as a quirk of hers, the first time he ever sat in this place. "That's where he told you."

"That's where he told us."

"He say anything else. Before he went."

"Just what I already told you. The road, the town, what he saw." A pause. "Getting it passed on to somebody who could do something with it. That's all he was staying for."

"So now what." It came out steadier than he expected. "It's still out there."

"Still there. Still doing whatever it's doing to whoever it's doing it to. That doesn't stop because Geoff got his answer."

A wraith. A word he'd have laughed at eleven months ago, filed next to alien abduction and government mind control. It didn't sound as stupid as it should have.

"How do you stop one."

Agnes's mouth did something that wasn't quite a smile. "That's the part they're better at than me."

She went still again, longer this time. "Hang on." A beat. "Okay. There's a bell. The real kind, not decoration. Something that actually does the job. If they had it, they'd have a real shot at putting this thing down for good."

"So get the bell."

"Not that easy." A longer pause. "Neither of them can touch it. Can't touch much of anything, that's just how they work now. Besides, it's not sitting around waiting to be borrowed. Last anybody heard, it's in a museum. Filed under something like Unknown Religious Object, if the last update's still good."

"Unknown Religious Object." Leo almost laughed and caught himself before it turned into one. "Somebody catalogued the thing that could've saved six lives as a paperweight."

"So it's useless."

"No." Agnes tilted her head, listening, and came back. "Just not something the four of us can go pick up ourselves."

"If somebody got it out of that museum," Leo said, working it through out loud, "and put it in your hands. That solves this."

"Not quite." She was speaking as herself now, not translating. He could hear the difference, her own cadence instead of someone else's. "Somebody still has to know how to ring it. That's not them. They know the theory. They can't do the part where a hand's involved."

"So somebody has to learn."

"Somebody would." A pause that ran longer than the sentence needed.

He could feel her not saying the rest of it, so he said it for her. "Somebody like me."

She didn't confirm it. She didn't have to.

"Alright." He was already standing, already reaching for the coat he didn't remember zipping back up. "Where's the museum."

Agnes blinked at him, the first genuinely surprised look he'd gotten out of her all night. "That's not what I meant. I wasn't asking you to volunteer."

"You didn't have to ask. I'm asking myself." He wasn't sure, saying it, whether this was the worst decision he'd made all year or the first good one. "We get the bell. I learn how to use it. We put this thing down before it takes anybody else off that road."

Agnes studied him a long moment, the kind of look with actual arithmetic behind it.

"You don't know the first thing about breaking into a museum."

"I know plenty about how museums get robbed. Worked burglary for two years." A beat. "Never done it from the other side. First time for everything."
