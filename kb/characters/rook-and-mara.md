# Rook & Mara

Joint page. Frequent hunter partners, not a couple — paired here because their working relationship, their perception, and the way their real backstories both surfaced in the same arc make them easiest to track together.

## Appearance

**Rook.** 43. Cropped brown hair, olive skin, hazel eyes. Broad-shouldered, built like a fighter — scars on his arms and hands, one on the side of his jaw. Mild New York Italian accent. Almost always in a leather jacket, often carrying a shotgun.

**Mara.** 46. Black hair with more silver in it than black already, shoulder-length — worn down unless she's on the move, when it goes into a low ponytail. Dark skin. Usually in tactical wear. Her tool of choice is a spool of silver chains she wields like a whip; she also carries a bag of potions, herbs, and other doodads she flatly refuses to explain.

## The hunter network, from the inside

Mara oversees the city for the network: assigns jobs, collects intel, has no visibility into other cities unless they make contact. See `world/hunter-network.md`. She and Rook learned about the ghosts and became secret allies; most of the network still doesn't know, since working with what hunters are meant to banish is controversial. Leo operates under Mara's jurisdiction without formal training, a status that traces back to `arcs/the-marlowe-case.md`.

## Rook's background

Surname **Manchetti**. City family, small apartment, parents Terry and Cathy, older brother Ross, younger sister Rosalind. When Rosalind was twelve, she and their father were caught in a category three incident: he was killed, she was blinded and crippled. She had told the family there was something in the hallway; seventeen-year-old Rook told her there was nothing there. Rosalind is faintly sensitive in her own right and always has been.

This background surfaced in **The Wishing House**, where the house builds Rook a room out of his real family, eight hundred square feet, 1998 — with Rosalind fourteen, sighted, and walking. The full room is the authoritative account of what happens there and what it costs him to leave; see `arcs/wishing-house/rooms.md` rather than duplicating it here.

## Mara's background

Born **Devlet Manoli**, into eleven generations of working witches — land craft, working craft, a good deal of it the kind the network confiscates or burns. Total recall for ritual structure, can hear a working done wrong from the next room, and has no magical aptitude whatsoever — tested at three, four, six, nine, eleven, and thirteen, nothing every time. There's a family word for it that doesn't translate; nearest is "cursed," but it means something closer to an unlucky thing that happens to a household. Nobody was cruel about it. She could not stay regardless.

Left at nineteen for the network, where the only question asked is whether the job got done. Has protected the family since: eleven hunter enquiries deflected in eight years, two falsified site reports. There's something under a stone lid in the family's yard, kept covered by a woodpile restacked twice a year — more often lately. She's known about it her whole life and never said a word.

This background surfaced in **The Wishing House**, in the room built from the Manoli seventh-year rite, where sixteen-year-old Devlet has the aptitude Mara never got. The room is also where the crew learns about the woodpile and Mara forces the issue of clearing it by proving her real standing with the family. Full account at `arcs/wishing-house/rooms.md`.

## After the Echo State / Spider arc

Both carry an unprovable instinct that whatever sets Sable and Aldric apart from other spirits traces back to Aldric's twenty years in the Hunger's dimension. No precedent exists to confirm it; they don't discuss it outside each other. It has moved their private read of the ghosts' danger ceiling upward without changing day-to-day trust at all. See `arcs/the-echo-state-the-spider.md` and `characters/sable-and-aldric.md`.

## Arc appearances

**The Marlowe Case** — Mara's introduction: catches sight of the real banishment bell hidden under Leo's jacket at the hunters' bar. Both help remove the entity from the haunted apartment and, on learning about the ghosts, choose to cover for them rather than hunt them. See `arcs/the-marlowe-case.md`.

**The Marcus Webb Case** — Rook is the one who identifies the twins afterward as true Seers. See `arcs/the-marcus-webb-case.md` and `characters/amelie-and-victor.md`.

**The Dream Eater** — Rook disrupts the illusion trapping the crew, on Sable and Aldric's engagement night, before it goes further. See `arcs/the-dream-eater.md`.

**The Julian Mission** *(arc-conditional)* — Arrive roughly thirty minutes after the panic signal to rescue Sable and Aldric from Julian. Also the pair who find and rescue Daniel Vega after his turning. See `arcs/the-julian-mission.md`.

**The Echo State / The Spider** *(arc-conditional)* — Ambushed and webbed by the spider; shielded by Sable and Aldric's solid bodies at the cost of the ghosts' echo state. See "After the Echo State / Spider arc," above, and `arcs/the-echo-state-the-spider.md`.

**The Vampire Coven / Valeria** *(arc-conditional)* — Work with Daniel Vega as his handlers throughout his undercover operation; take down the combat types in the final confrontation. See `arcs/the-vampire-coven-valeria.md`.

**The Alegrove Island Murders** — Rook is cast as the Colonel (blowdart to the neck, north overlook); Mara as the Magistrate (shot from the lawn through the drawing-room window). See `arcs/alegrove-island-murders/overview.md` and `arcs/alegrove-island-murders/roster.md`.

**The Wishing House** — See "Rook's background" and "Mara's background," above, and the full room-by-room account at `arcs/wishing-house/rooms.md`.

**The Lich's Wheel** *(alt-timeline)* — Two of the four living crew pulled through into the alternate past. Privately recognize the alternate Captain/Ritualist dynamic as their own hunter "cleansing-focus" burnout pattern. See `arcs/lichs-wheel/overview.md`.
