# Leo Castello

Private investigator, ex-police. The crew's cop instincts, and the only member of the standing cast made partially sensitive by sheer repetition rather than a single triggering event.

## Background

Former cop; left the force after digging into a cold case without authorization. Runs private investigations now, with a reputation for solving strange ones — which is how the bakery found him, or he found it. See `arcs/leo-castello-introduction.md`.

As of the Introduction arc's opening he is the better part of a year off the force, not three months.

A great detective who brings a cop's instincts to dangerous cases, for better and worse — his tunnel-vision drive to solve a case is a genuine asset and, in at least one arc, the whole reason he survives something nobody else could have.

## Appearance

43. Short, wavy black hair that always looks a bit messy. Tall and fit. Perpetually tired eyes with dark circles, stubble.

Almost always in a shirt and chino pants, plus a jacket with a lot of inside pockets — practical rather than stylish. Wears a holster for the replica bell (see Perception, below) and occasionally a sidearm, a leftover habit from his cop days.

## Perception

Carries a replica banishment bell (see `world/objects.md`) — unblessed iron, stuns and repels, banishes nothing, exists to buy him seconds to get out of a room. Carrying and ringing it case after case knocked partial sensitivity loose in him: he hears and sees Sable and Aldric, less clearly than Agnes does. See `world/perception-tiers.md`.

## Voice

Third person, past tense, close on Leo. Free indirect: his judgment sits in the narration without a tag, so the prose can be exasperated without a narrator standing outside him commenting on it. Settled 2026-08-29 across two pilots, `work/drafts/leo-voice-pilot-b-third-past.md` (hostile stranger, on the job) and `work/drafts/leo-voice-pilot-c-agnes-bakery.md` (off duty, with a friend).

**He solves things nobody asked him to solve, he is right, and being right makes the room worse.** This is the voice; the tiredness, the persistence and the exasperation all run off it. He is not chasing a win. He cannot leave a thread alone, he is very good at pulling it, and it costs him something every time. Write the cost, not the cleverness.

**Reference point:** David Mills from *Se7en*, if Mills had gotten ten peaceful years on the force under a Somerset before it went wrong, mellowed a little, and then ended up solo. The ambition sanded down into grievance with the world's sloppiness. The mentor is deliberately offstage and unnamed — see `open-questions.md`.

**Guard is the variable, and it moves with company.** Across a desk from someone lying to him he is clipped and interrogative, sentences short, questions punctuated as statements. With people he likes he runs long, interrupts himself, gets physical, laughs louder than the room, and drops closer to a younger Mills. Same man, different amount of guard.

The most voice-defining block from either pilot, from the bakery scene:

> "Seriously?" Leo sat back. "That's insane. That's an insane way to spend an afterlife. Four hundred years and neither of you can go look, and you're still..." He gestured at the corner, and the gesture got away from him a little. "You know there are people who'd kill for that kind of time. You know that, right? You two? Over there? People would kill."

**"Seriously?" is not a catchphrase.** He says it out loud once in that block, as a release after the pressure has built. It works because it is the only time. Twice in a chapter and it is a tic.

**Not yet established:** what he sounds like frightened, and what he sounds like when the persistence fails and he cannot solve the thing. Neither pilot tested either. Don't extrapolate; ask.

## Standing with the hunters

Functions as a hunter under Mara's jurisdiction without formal training — a status acquired by accident during The Marlowe Case (see `arcs/the-marlowe-case.md`). Some early tension came with it; once Rook and Mara learned about the ghosts and chose to cover for them, the three became permanent allies. See `characters/rook-and-mara.md` and `world/hunter-network.md`.

## Arc appearances

**Leo Castello — Introduction** — Brought a cold case to Agnes, learned it was supernatural and still active, and was the one who proposed stealing the banishment bell from the museum to solve it. Became a bakery fixture afterward. See `arcs/leo-castello-introduction.md`.

**Museum Theft Aftermath** — Carries the replica bell in the field ever since; the real bell's theft became a famous unsolved case that never resurfaces publicly. See `arcs/museum-theft-aftermath.md`.

**The Marlowe Case** — Went with Agnes to the hunters' bar to reach Rook, which is where Mara first appears (and first clocks the real bell hidden under his jacket). Effectively became a hunter under Mara's jurisdiction in the process. See `arcs/the-marlowe-case.md`.

**Cora Winstead — The Frame-Up** — Took Cora's case and brought Sable and Aldric to the interview cell, which is how Cora's own mediumship came out. See `arcs/cora-winstead-the-frame-up.md`. This arc is also where Chen figures out something supernatural is going on; see `characters/chen.md`.

**The Alegrove Island Murders** — Cast as the Detective (1934 identity: Ray Graves). The only crew member the house lets remember between cycles — the exception it grants because E.W.'s request for the truth is the first good-faith one it's ever received. Holds all five loops of memory, including four murders committed by his own hand in loop three to isolate the ghost variable, and writes the whole case up in a private notebook he doesn't share with the others. See `arcs/alegrove-island-murders/overview.md` and `arcs/alegrove-island-murders/loop-progression.md`.

**The Wishing House** *(arc-conditional)* — His room is the dinner: the house offers him his family intact and asks him to simply sit down and eat before it gives him the key back. Tells his mother, in-scene, the hard true thing about why he became a cop and why he isn't one anymore. See `arcs/wishing-house/rooms.md`.

**The Lich's Wheel** *(alt-timeline)* — One of the four living crew pulled through with Sable and Aldric into the alternate past. See `arcs/lichs-wheel/overview.md`.
