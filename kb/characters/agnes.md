# Agnes

Baker. The living hinge of the crew — the one every other thread runs through to reach the world outside the bakery.

## Background

Descends from a bakery Sable and Aldric frequented during Sable's cleric days; the present-day bakery's bread recipe is close enough to that original recipe that it's what drew the ghosts back in the first place. See `arcs/the-bakery-agnes.md` for how that first contact actually happened.

A sensitive. Not born to it — a triggering event sharpened her sight (see Arc appearances, below). Sees Sable and Aldric easily; other ghosts only with effort. See `world/perception-tiers.md` for the full tier system: Agnes sits above the partial sensitives (Leo, Rook, Mara, the twins) and below a born medium like Cora.

## Appearance

39, present day. Dirty blonde hair, almost always up in a bun — she wore it down more often back when she first met Sable and Aldric, in her late twenties/early thirties (which also roughly dates The Bakery / Agnes arc to about seven to ten years before the present). Brown eyes, a face that blends easily into a crowd. On the shorter side, surprisingly athletic — probably from all the baking.

Wears a dress that's easy to move in, plus an apron, almost always. Partial to light blue, so a lot of her wardrobe runs that color.

## Current role

The ghosts' front and their bridge to the living world. Where Sable and Aldric can't be seen, can't sign for a delivery, can't talk to a client across the counter, Agnes can. She also holds custody of Cookie — day-to-day care, not really control; nobody controls Cookie. See `characters/cookie.md`.

## Voice

**She asks questions like a person, with question marks.** Agnes is plain and unhurried, and she is not performing non-reaction. Her flatness is warmth held steady, not affect withheld. If her dialogue is coming out as flat declaratives, that is the chapter 5 failure repeating — see `../issues/prose-tics.md`.

**Emotion is audible in her punctuation and sentence shape**, not only described in the narration around the line. When she is scared, straining, or out of patience the line itself carries it: a break, a repeated word, an interruption, a register that slips. "Leo, *move*."

**The relay is a structure, not a punctuation trick.** When Agnes translates for the ghosts she stays plain, and she chooses what to quote directly and what to summarize. That choice is where the meaning is: a line she decides to give verbatim carries weight because she could have paraphrased it and didn't, and a line she declines to relay says something about her, not just about the speaker. Don't render the ghosts' quoted lines in a heightened register she wouldn't use herself — exclamation marks in her quotation marks are a leftover from the removed style files, not a real device. She also hedges and self-corrects when relaying in real time, because she is genuinely unsure she's getting it right; that is her specific hesitation pattern, not generic filler.

**She talks the whole way through a lock.** See The younger days, below.

## The younger days

Agnes picks locks, and she is very good at it — not hobbyist-good, practiced-good. She keeps her own picks in a flat leather case in her apron pocket, worn soft at the fold, with a bright patch on the tension wrench where her thumb has ridden it for years. She works by feel rather than by sight and talks the whole way through.

Her only explanation, when asked, is that she was **wild in her younger days**. She does not elaborate, and pressing her doesn't work.

First shown on the page in `work/drafts/book1-chapter-05.md`, where she opens the museum's service door on the way in and relocks it on the way out — which is a large part of why the theft is never solved (see `arcs/museum-theft-aftermath.md`).

**This is deliberately unresolved and meant to stay that way for now.** The author's intent is that it gets expanded in bits and pieces across later arcs rather than answered in one scene. Don't close it; don't hint toward a specific answer.

## Vulnerabilities and stakes

Agnes has no supernatural defenses of her own — no bell, no hunter training, no ward. Her exposure is social and emotional rather than physical: she's the one who has to sit with not knowing, more than once (see Arc appearances). She's also the one person whose absence an arc can be built around, because she's the crew member most likely to notice and hold a thread others would drop.

Note that her role as translator makes her structurally load-bearing in any scene where a non-sensitive has to act on what the ghosts know. In `work/drafts/book1-chapter-05.md` this is the whole tension of the heist: the ghosts track the guard, Agnes is the only one who hears them, and Leo can't act on anything she doesn't relay in time.

## Arc appearances

**The Bakery / Agnes** — Present when Rook shot the ghosts with salt; already mildly sensitive, the blast unlocked full clarity. Tracked the injured ghosts to a warehouse and offered them bread rather than fear. Talked Rook down and got him to call off future hunts on Sable and Aldric. Invited the ghosts to haunt the bakery — the founding act of the whole present-day arrangement. See `arcs/the-bakery-agnes.md`.

**Leo Castello — Introduction** — Revealed the ghosts to Leo, then went into the museum with him and handled every lock on the job. See `arcs/leo-castello-introduction.md`.

**Cookie's True Nature** — Took in Cookie at Mr. Patterson's ghost's request, not yet knowing what she was. Present for the reveal of Cookie's true nature. See `arcs/cookies-true-nature.md` and `characters/cookie.md`.

**The Holy Rot / Cookie's Void** — Believes, for a full week, that Cookie has eaten and killed Sable and Aldric — she has no reason to think otherwise. Goes through denial, bargaining (asked Cookie for a sign and got the "cuddly era" instead), and a real grief cry before coming out the other side and going back to work. Cookie knows the whole time that they're coming back; Agnes doesn't. See `arcs/the-holy-rot-cookies-void.md`.

**The Wishing House** *(arc-conditional)* — Specifically **absent** from this arc. It requires the living to be unable to perceive the ghosts, and Agnes is the one crew member who could break that condition just by being there, so the arc's own logic keeps her out. See `arcs/wishing-house/overview.md`.

**The Alegrove Island Murders** — Cast as the Cook: the first to die, first night, poisoned in her sleep. Her death is what strands the rest of the crew in a forty-room house with no staff and no food they can find, since she was the only one who knew the building. See `arcs/alegrove-island-murders/overview.md`.

**The Lich's Wheel** *(alt-timeline)* — One of the four living crew pulled through with Sable and Aldric. Talks the group past a hostile stop of period-armed riders using a badly-improvised attempt at period speech. See `arcs/lichs-wheel/overview.md`.
