# Aura Chronicle — Cast

*[BACKGROUND]* All figures below are centuries dead; nothing here changes. See `overview.md` for the throughline and how this connects to present-day arcs.

## Aura

Youngest of Osric and Agatha's eight children — Aldric's aunt, born a generation before Marta's son and closest in practice to Marta, who effectively raised her. A genuine sensitive from early childhood: she sees and speaks with minor nature spirits invisible to everyone else, and can coax plants to grow unnaturally fast. The village reads it first as an odd child's imagination, then, as Father Ansel's suspicion hardens, as witchcraft. At twelve, brought to the chapterhouse for evaluation, she refuses to enter, screaming that something under the building is awake and starving — accurate, as the Origin arc later confirms, though it only deepens Ansel's suspicion at the time.

Shielded for years by Marta and her six brothers, she leaves the village at sixteen, partly to stop hiding and partly to free her siblings from the cost of covering for her. Alone in the hills she meets, names, and eventually strikes a permanent blood pact with the deity she calls Lamby — a pact that marks her a warlock to any trained cleric for the rest of her life. A season with a hunter named Joran leaves her pregnant with her daughter, Nettle; Joran is gone before he learns.

When the pestilence Lamby has suppressed for centuries breaks loose, Aura accepts Lamby's pact to ward her own land, at the cost of her family's protection — the fever that follows kills her parents' entire remaining household and most of the region, including Marta and the son Aura will never know survived. Aura believes her whole birth family died in the fever's mass grave for the next twenty years, until secondhand rumor of a dead young cleric named Aldric reaches her and she puts together, without ever getting to confirm it fully, that her nephew lived long enough to die anyway. She never learns he survived as a ghost.

She spends the rest of her long life (she dies at 86) rebuilding: raising Nettle and then grandchildren and great-grandchildren, keeping careful watch on the church to avoid discovery, teaching what can't be inherited as lived sensitivity as rote tradition instead. Her relationship to the church never resolves cleanly — raised devout, driven out personally rather than ideologically, and aware to the end that the same institution that hunted her also raised her orphaned nephew with real gentleness before using him as an unwilling sacrifice. She holds both without needing one to cancel the other.

She is the sole ancestor of every present-day Fischer, including Hans. No living Fischer remembers her by name.

## Lamby

An ancient nature deity, roughly lamb-shaped, that has held the same stretch of northern hills and suppressed the pestilence entity there alone for longer than the village has existed. Neither good nor evil; genuinely drawn to human company, and approves specifically of what shepherds do for a flock, since Aura is one herself. Aura names it as a joke about mortal audacity — naming something older than the church — and it's amused enough to keep the name permanently.

Over years it teaches Aura real nature-magic: true names, intent, workings that hold. When the pestilence breaks loose, Lamby offers the only protection it can — a permanent blood pact that wards Aura's land specifically, sealed with ribbons and bells tied into its horns and a cut across Aura's palm. The cost to Lamby is permanent: it diminishes itself into the physical shape of an ordinary lamb to share enough of itself to ward the land, and can never extend protection beyond that land, not even to Aura's birth family.

It remains with Aura's descendants for four centuries afterward — present at nearly every birth, wedding, and burial, known to the living Fischers only as "the old lamb." In the present day it's still suppressing the pestilence on a fragile balance that, in the shelved `archive/the-pestilence-the-lamb.md` material, eventually fails badly enough to require Hans Fischer's intervention — that arc is no longer part of the active story, so as canon stands the balance simply continues to hold. No living Fischer has ever known what it actually is; Lamby still recognizes Aura's family resemblance in them regardless.

## The pestilence entity

Ancient and native to the northern hills, closer to a force of nature than a monster — indifferent rather than malevolent. Lamby has held it down for longer than any human settlement in the region existed. It breaks loose once, catastrophically, provoked by an unrelated and unrecorded working attempted in a neighboring village, and sweeps the region: lambs first, then the youngest and oldest villagers, killing most of the region including Aura's birth family and, generations later, forming the same recurring threat behind Aldric's own family's death.

Aura and Lamby together suppress smaller flare-ups for years afterward. At some inferred but never dramatized point, the two of them bind the weakened entity into a cave barrier keyed specifically to Aura's bloodline — which is why, in the shelved material, only a Fischer descendant (Hans) is able to open that barrier when the entity surges again and wounds Lamby badly enough for the fever to bleed through once more. See `archive/the-pestilence-the-lamb.md` for that (no longer active) present-day return.

## Nettle

Aura's daughter, born of Aura's season with Joran and named jointly by Aura and Marta during Marta's visit for the birth. Shows real, if weaker, sensitivity from age four — she can hear Lamby directly, if faintly, and befriends it in her own right. Survives the fever behind her mother's wards, marries into a neighboring family at 22, and becomes the line through which every present-day Fischer descends. Dies decades after Aura, of old age.

## Joel

Nettle's husband, a local man. Otherwise a minor figure in the Chronicle proper — his one notable moment (recognizing danger and running ahead of a militant cleric to warn the homestead) belongs to the alternate-timeline Lich's Wheel material, not this canon.

## Marta

Aldric's mother, Aura's oldest sister, and effectively Aura's day-to-day parent given their age gap. The closest sibling to Aura after her exile, maintaining secret correspondence for years primarily through Bram. Rides out to the hills while pregnant, against Aura's own request, to be present for Nettle's birth, and stays through it and a fortnight after. Dies in the fever alongside her husband, using her last strength to tell twelve-year-old Aldric that he has an aunt somewhere north and west — without enough detail for him to ever find her, and unaware Aura will spend the next twenty years believing her whole family already dead.

## Osric & Agatha

Aura's parents, shepherds in the unnamed northern-hills village. Neither survives to see the fever: Osric dies of old age when Nettle is six, Agatha the following spring, some years before the pestilence breaks loose.

## The six brothers — Wendel, Bram, Corwin, Halvard, Tobin, Garrick

Aura's brothers, born between Marta and Aura. As children, they organize with Marta to shield Aura from the village and the church, covering for her at real and sustained cost to themselves for years. Bram in particular carries secret correspondence and supplies to Aura throughout her exile, primarily via traveling peddlers and drovers. All six marry and have children of their own; all six die in the fever along with most of those children. Corwin's chin and Bram's laugh are among the features Aura later recognizes resurfacing in her grandchildren and great-grandchildren.

## Father Ansel

The village priest. Grows suspicious of Aura from her childhood and is the one who reports her to the Dawnchurch chapterhouse, setting in motion the evaluation she refuses to enter at twelve. His suspicion never resolves into anything more than that — he dies among the first adults taken by the fever, decades after Aura has already left.

## Joran

A hunter, lost and injured, who stumbles onto Aura's homestead during her exile. The two are involved for a season; he never learns the extent of what she is until he witnesses a small working done without thinking, and leaves without a word the next morning. He is never traced or heard from again, and doesn't appear to have reported her, though Aura never learns why or where he went. He is Nettle's father, and never learns Aura was pregnant.
