# Objects and devices

## The banishment bell

An old technique of the Dawnchurch's militant branch. Requires iron of a specific design, tuned to a precise resonance, plus the skill to ring it correctly — knowledge lost to time. Every cleric was trained on the bells regardless of branch. See `world/church-history.md`.

**The true bell.** The only one known to exist. Stolen from a museum during Leo's first case (see `arcs/leo-castello-introduction.md` and `arcs/museum-theft-aftermath.md`) and hidden at the bakery ever since. It genuinely banishes. Heavy — a two-handed, planted-feet proposition, not something anyone carries — and dangerous to Sable and Aldric as well as to whatever it's aimed at (see What it does to Sable and Aldric, below). It comes out only when the crew means to banish something and the something warrants it.

Sable and Aldric can both ring it correctly, if not to a ritualist's standard — but neither can lift it without the emitter (below). **Practical consequence:** the only two people who know how to use the true bell are the two it's most dangerous to, and everyone else on the crew is working from their instruction rather than training.

### The two branches' bells

**Militant branch: the large bell.** The surviving true bell is one of these. Roughly fourteen inches, black iron gone brown in patches, thick through the wall, undecorated except a band of shallow marks around the crown that could be writing and could be damage. Carrying it is a two-armed, chest-height, no-free-hands proposition that makes stairs and doors a real problem. This is the one that banishes.

**Archival branch: hand bells, worn on the belt.** Roughly a third the size, drawn one-handed and rung where the cleric stood. **Weaker.** This is what Sable and Aldric trained on and carried in life; neither of them had ever handled a militant one, which is why the true bell's weight surprises everyone in the room, them included.

**The line this draws to Leo's replica.** The replica Leo eventually carries in a holster is, in shape and scale, an archival hand bell — a one-handed object that stuns and buys seconds rather than a two-handed one that puts something down for good. The gap between the two branches' bells is the same gap as between the true bell and the replica.

### What it does to Sable and Aldric

The bell does not distinguish between its target and anything else present. **It does not unmake them.** A ring is an injury, not an annihilation: the ghost equivalent of being hit by a truck. Badly hurt, out of commission, a long recovery, and no use to anybody for the duration of it.

**Severity scales with proximity.** The closer they are when it sounds, the worse it lands. Across a room is survivable and awful. Standing beside it is worse. Nobody has established what holding it would do, and nobody intends to find out.

This is the constraint that shapes every scene involving the bell: whoever rings it has to do it with Sable and Aldric as far away as the situation allows, which usually means the two people who know how to use it correctly are the two who have to be somewhere else when it happens.

**Clapper discipline.** Standard practice for moving it, worked out by the crew during the museum job: wrap and tape the clapper *before* the bell moves at all, and keep Sable and Aldric clear regardless. The wrap reduces the risk; it does not remove it.

### Leo's replica

Unblessed iron, right shape, wrong resonance. Stuns and repels, banishes nothing. A tool for buying seconds — and the repeated exposure that knocked partial sensitivity loose in Leo. Made after the museum job, not before. Sized and carried like an archival hand bell (see above). See `characters/leo-castello.md` and `world/perception-tiers.md`.

## The emitter

*(Arc-conditional. Six arcs require it to exist — see `arcs/index.md` for the full dependency list.)*

Stephanie Constable's device, built out of the corrupted CT-scan data in `arcs/yuki-stephanie-and-the-emitter.md`. Makes ghosts solid, tangible, and visible for a few hours per charge.

**Solid-form tells:** dilated pupils, cold skin, no pulse, breathing that's voluntary and sometimes falls into unnatural rhythm. Dogs know something's wrong; cats don't care.

**Blood.** In solid form, blood is spirit energy made liquid — see `world/ghost-mechanics.md` for what that means when a ghost takes a serious wound in solid form.
