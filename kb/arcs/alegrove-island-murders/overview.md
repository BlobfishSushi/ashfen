# The Alegrove Island Murders

A time-loop murder mystery. An unsolved 1934 mass murder replays on an isolated island estate. The house casts seven new arrivals as the seven original dead, overwrites their memories completely, and runs the massacre on the same schedule. It has done this for eighty-eight years.

Leo alone remembers between cycles. The loop breaks when a guest declares the full truth of 1934 aloud so all seven hear it, which can only happen before the first death. It takes Leo five loops.

## What carries out

- Leo holds five loops of memory nobody else has, including four murders in his own hand (loop 3), and a written record he does not share.
- The others retain a fading impression of the fifth loop only: being someone, a man saying things that turned out to be true, a shadow coming apart, a plea about a child.

## The hook

A letter from a dying old woman signing only "E.W." She is Elaine Winsley, the child the Physician saved decades earlier by smothering her abusive father, though she never learned that. She knows only that Liam Sondheim, the one person ever kind to her, went to an island with six others and never came back. She wants to know what happened before she runs out of time.

Leo brings the letter to the bakery. All seven come; only five are visible to the ferryman. On arrival the island holds a decrepit, long-abandoned mansion. As Leo climbs the front steps, the world shifts, and he opens his eyes at the same door, restored, mid-rainstorm.

E.W.'s significance to the loop: eighty-eight years of guests have come through. She is the first outside party to ask the house, in good faith and with standing, for the truth. That request is what makes the house yield its one exception, a detective who remembers.

## The truth

The Physician, Liam Sondheim, was the massacre's architect. His motive: avenging a man driven to suicide by the original Detective's investigation, and punishing the rest as killers who had never faced consequence. He had killed once before (smothering Elaine's father) and considered it justified.

He faked his own death mid-massacre with a measured dose of a datura-and-monkshood tincture, was examined and covered, and thereafter not counted by anyone. He continued killing and took a lethal dose of the same tincture last. The body was found in the same room by the same means as the false death.

Two of the seven deaths were not his work: the Detective was shot by the Actress (who had built a sound case against him), and the Physician's final dose was suicide.

## The ghost anomaly

Sable and Aldric are the house's two worst casting choices. It cast the performer and the healer without accounting for either being dead already. They die on schedule in 1934 and never reach the ruins; only Leo can perceive their ghosts. In an uninterfered loop, Leo never meets them because both die after he does.

The anomaly that cracks the case: across loops 1-2, Aldric dies in the study on schedule and his ghost appears nowhere. In loop 3, Leo kills Aldric out of position and Aldric's ghost appears immediately. This rules out method, order, room, and hand, leaving only what they are. But if that were the answer, Aldric would appear in loops 1-2 too. The resolution: in loops 1-2 Aldric did not die. The study death is the Physician's false one. Nobody comes back from a death that didn't happen.
