# The Rooms

The authoritative full account of each room the crew passes through. Several rooms surface major character backstory, Rook's real family in Room 1 and Mara's Devlet Manoli origin in Room 2 above all, and this file is where that backstory lives in full. `characters/rook-and-mara.md` links back here rather than duplicating it; treat this as the source when the two disagree.

No guide exists through the house. Tobias stays in the last room throughout, and the crew works the whole building on faith that he's still in it. See `overview.md` for the operating mechanics (Cora's wish, decaying ghost effects, the house learning) that hold across every room below.

## Room 0: Cora. The kitchen

Cora at six, eating cereal at a table with her back to the hall doorway, which the real Cora could never do, because a man stood in that doorway every morning for five years. He isn't there. The child's attention crosses the room without catching on anything.

Cora works out what the room is and admits she wanted this until she was thirty-one, and that she'd take the man in the doorway back if it meant seeing Sable and Aldric again. Exit: a storage closet with a mop in it.

## Room 1: Rook. The apartment

The Manchettis, eight hundred square feet, 1998. Ross, Rook at fifteen, their father Terry, their mother Cathy, and **Rosalind at fourteen, walking and sighted**. In the real world, Rosalind and their father were caught in a category three when she was twelve; he died, she was blinded and crippled.

Rosalind is faintly sensitive and Cora's wish doesn't hold on her fully. She can tell something is in the corner by the radiator, and puts her hand through the edge of Aldric. Rook, who heard that exact sentence out of a twelve-year-old three days before the accident, draws his weapon, knowing it may be Aldric and unable to tell. The ghosts retreat, betting the thing that took his family never did that, and Rook reads it as intel.

Rook tells his family the truth outright: he's Rook from the future, this is supernatural, it's mostly handled. They let the crew search. The exit is a fire escape with a broken ladder and a four-storey drop onto a dumpster lid that opens, relayed through Rosalind since only she can hear the ghosts scouting it. Rook hugs his family, his father and sister especially, promises to visit his mother and siblings when he's out, and jumps first. The family watch four strangers throw themselves one by one into a bin.

## Room 2: Mara. The long house

The Manoli seventh-year rite. Sixteen-year-old **Devlet Manoli** stands in the chalk and the fire goes out: she has the aptitude, in this room's version of her life.

This is where the crew learns Mara comes from eleven generations of working witches and was born with none. Devlet reads the gear the crew is carrying (bell, salt, bloodline reader) and calls hunters on the house. Mara puts her weapons on the floor and recites the seventh-year rite word-perfect, including the closing, and nothing happens; the family accepts her on the strength of it anyway.

The exit is under a stone lid in the yard, stacked over with a cord and a half of firewood that gets restacked twice a year, more often lately. Devlet refuses to clear it. Mara forces the issue by proving she's family: naming the fictional brother every Manoli child learns at seven, and stating that she has protected the real cellar for thirty years and falsified two site reports to do it. The family clears the wood. What's under the lid is never shown, and the relatives make clear it's very bad. Nobody asks. Leo says "later" and means it.

## Room 3: Leo. The dinner

A Wednesday, chicken, both parents home, his sister drawing a horse and not caring about anything. Nine-year-old Leo has the front door key on a string and names his price: sit down and eat first, then the key.

This is the house's cleverest move. The crew have broken three rooms at a run because running is how they survive it, so it offers the slow version instead. All four sit. Leo convinces two sharp cops he's their son from the future using details only he'd have, pulls two extra chairs for the ghosts, and is interrogated over dinner by parents who never repeat a question. He tells his mother the truth: that his parents were good people doing a good job, that there was nobody to be angry at, that this was all he ever wanted and he had six of them. He mentions he became a cop and got put off the force; his father's response is *was you right?* The boy hands the key over without asking again.

## Room 4: Aldric. The farmhouse

The house recognises the ghosts' own wishes are too stale to hold (see `overview.md` on decay), and stops offering them one, starting instead to bait: it builds the room for the ghosts and aims it at the crew.

The crew drop through a hay hatch into a barn; forty sheep bolt. Aldric's family are alive: his mother **Marta**, his uncles **Corwin** and **Bram**, and Aldric at sixteen wrestling a ewe, with a flock tally in his head and a controlled trial running on a sheep drench. This is not the childhood village: in this version there was no fever, and the family moved west two years ago to be nearer Aura. The uncles speak only the hill dialect; Marta and the boy have town English and translate. The ghosts are solid here, though nobody but the crew can perceive them.

Marta feeds the strangers, accepts "lost, friends, want to look at doors" as sufficient, and sends the boy round the house with them. None of the doors work. She spots the bell in Leo's coat, notices all four strangers watching her son like someone laid out, and rides for Aura without telling anyone.

**Aura in this timeline** never had to make the pact, since the fever never came, so she is powerful, self-taught, and unmarked. She counts six people in a room of four before her gloves are off, and pins the ghosts with a powder that overrides Cora's wish and makes them visible to everyone sensitive. Mara recognises a working woman instantly and stands down the room. Aura interrogates the ghosts in a compulsion tongue neither recognises, older than the church, older than the language they died speaking, and gets their names, their deaths, and four hundred years of drifting. She releases them on the grounds that nobody should have to live like that.

She lies to the boy, calling them lost spirits, handled, nothing to worry about, and takes Marta to the dairy to tell her the truth: that these are travellers from another world and forward of it, that two of them are dead, that one of them is her son at twenty-four, and that whatever happened to him cannot happen to hers. She refuses to say how he died. The boy eavesdrops from the dairy wall and infers most of it, then goes to Cora, who confirms it and tells him about the bakery and the cases instead of the death.

Mara and Aura sit up until two in the morning: total recall and no aptitude against extreme aptitude and no training, neither of whom has ever had anyone to talk to. Rook is conscripted into a difficult lambing. Leo watches a sixteen-year-old be sixteen.

Aura cuts a door in the barn wall at first light; the whole household turns out for it. Marta asks where the ghosts are standing, guesses wrong by four feet, and says goodbye anyway: that he was fed and warm and thoroughly loved every day he was in that house. Leo ruffles the boy's hair on the way past. Aldric doesn't look back.

## Room 5: Sable. The chapterhouse

The house goes for the throat: the east archive, no Hunger, no Maren, **Pell and Tem alive and well**. The ghosts are solid again and read to everyone as ordinary clerics. Rook notes the building is the cleanest room he's ever stood in, no background wrongness at all, because there's nothing under it.

Tem walks in, complains about the pair slacking, hands them books to restack, and assumes the strangers are visiting scholars. Sable hugs her instead of taking the books. Pell comes in, pats Aldric on the shoulder in passing, notices they both look grey, and marches them to the kitchen to be fed. Sable refuses to explain and Pell lets her, on the grounds that they're twenty-six and twenty-four and entitled to something he isn't told, but says he'll check again after compline.

When he does, he reaches for Sable's wrist out of habit and she pulls her hand back. Aldric ends it: he crouches by the chair and puts Pell's hand on his own wrist. Pell checks the wrist, the neck, then Sable's, and asks three questions: are you in danger, are you a danger to anyone here, is there anything I can do. He accepts all three answers and stops asking. Sable tells Tem nothing, wanting the few hours they have to be quiet ones.

None of the doors work. Pell then mentions a sealed passage behind the third bay that nobody but he knows about, and takes them himself; Tem comes along, having wanted in there her whole life. At the bottom of the stair is a small stone room, a crypt cut for the house and never used, six empty niches, nothing that has ever happened in it. In the middle of the floor, standing free, is a plain wooden door with light under it. No iron, no nails, no significance.

**In the real timeline this is the room where the sealing happened**, and that is the door Aldric died at and both of them were pulled through. Here it's a door in an empty cellar, and they walk past it. Pell and Tem have no idea. Before they go, Pell kisses both of them on the forehead the way he did when they were small. Tem, with no context at all, feels it as a goodbye and can't say why.

## Room 6: Tobias

No wish left to show. The house is the same house, furnished, and Tobias is on the living room floor with his parents, who died on the ring road not long ago.

He's furious: they walked out of every single one, have they got no heart? He isn't fooled and says so: he's counted **four hundred and six Tuesdays** and knows exactly where his father's laugh lands. He's decided, not deceived.

He asks the ghosts directly and can't hear them, so he asks Rook, who walked away from his father and a sister who could see. Rook's answer is that he didn't choose to leave: two people he couldn't hear opened a window slowly so he'd watch, and got him out of a room he was never leaving. Leo tells the truth and it's a bad answer: his own mother is alive in Florida and he rings her on Sundays. Sable, relayed word for word through Cora, admits she'd have stayed if it had been only her, and that the one thing that moved her was Agnes not knowing where they were.

The house intervenes through the parents, gentle, reasonable, never raising its voice, *you don't know any better, front door's where it always was*, and opens the real front door for the first time in the arc. Nobody takes it.

Cora makes the two arguments that land. First the count: four hundred and six identical Tuesdays is knowing a day, not having one. Then the aunt: Aldric had an aunt he never met, nothing at all like his mother, and she was extraordinary, and a different shape of life is not the same as a worse one. Backed by the crew flatly refusing to leave without him, Tobias steps out from under his mother's arm.

**Resolution.** They walk him out into a Thursday afternoon. The records restore themselves on the drive: the file whole, the aunt on it three times as the person who filed and forgot eleven times. On the pavement she admits she'll be rubbish at this for a couple of years. Tobias asks if she's cool. She says she's never had anyone to be cool at, but she could probably manage it. That settles it and he goes inside.
