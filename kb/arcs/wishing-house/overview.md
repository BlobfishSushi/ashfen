# The Wishing House

A house that grants childhood wishes, not the wish you have now but the one you had when you were small, and then argues to keep you. It has done this since long before the crew arrives, and does not fight to hold anyone; it persuades.

## Position and dependencies

Anywhere after the crew is assembled. Requires Leo, Cora, Rook, Mara, Sable, and Aldric. No emitter — the crew doesn't bring or use one for this job, not because it can't exist yet. This isn't locked to before Yuki, Stephanie, and the Emitter; it can sit any time after, as long as the case doesn't read as one that calls for solidifying the ghosts going in. A missing nine-year-old last seen at a condemned house plays as a routine search, not a job anyone would think to prep gear for.

**Agnes must be absent.** The whole arc runs on the living being unable to perceive Sable and Aldric once inside (see *Core mechanics*, below), and Agnes is the one crew member who'd break that: as a sensitive, she sees the ghosts easily under ordinary circumstances, so her presence would give the house's central pressure nowhere to bite. See `world/perception-tiers.md` for where Agnes sits relative to everyone else.

## The hook

Tobias Kerr, nine, goes missing; last seen at a long-abandoned house on Cora's condemned-buildings list. Cora surveyed it years ago and picked up nothing at all. Chen has a missing persons report where the name and photograph are gone from the file (the record exists, the subject doesn't), gets the bad feeling, and hands it to Leo. Leo can't find a single document about the house anywhere and calls Rook and Mara for backup.

Six go in. They find Tobias immediately, and he won't leave: the house is better, here his wishes are true. When they try to take him out the front door, the door opens onto somewhere else.

## Core mechanics, in brief

**Cora's wish is the operating condition.** The first wish the house ever took was Cora's, at six: not to be able to see. It granted that one across the whole building, permanently, to everyone inside. Nobody living can perceive Sable or Aldric while they're in the house; Leo's bell rings and does nothing; charms go inert.

**The ghosts' effects survive but decay.** Cold spots, flickers, moving small objects: all weak from the start and weaker the longer the ghosts stay, until they're down to managing one knock where they began able to write whole sentences in scattered flour. Every use costs, so partway through they have to start choosing which of two things is worth saying.

**The house learns.** Each room is harder to leave than the last, because it watched how the crew got the previous one out. It doesn't trap; the exits are refusable, not hidden. It argues, and it gets better at arguing.
