# The Lich's Wheel

An alternate-timeline arc, complete in itself. The crew is pulled four hundred years into a diverged past, not a branch of the present-day world, by an artifact rather than by choice. Nothing here alters home-timeline canon except the crew's memories and the wheel itself, which comes home with them.

## Position and dependencies

Self-contained; no crew-assembly requirement beyond the six who go through (Rook, Mara, Agnes, Leo, Sable, Aldric). No emitter present on either side of the trip.

## The trigger

Rook and Mara brought a cartwheel-sized wooden wheel to the bakery, recovered by the hunter network: stone-mounted, bone-inlaid, symbols too old and too northern-hills for the network's archivists to read. Sable and Aldric spent three hours decoding it: a lich's escape hatch, a one-way gate for undead energy, incomplete, the destination ring bare, no target marked. They concluded it was inert without a spoken trigger and an active target, and missed a worn override clause near the hub: absent a marked destination, the wheel defaults to "the nearest safe alternative" for undead energy.

Sable read the trigger phrase aloud, reflexively, while translating. The wheel activated, grabbed the nearest undead energy (Sable and Aldric), and pulled the four living crew standing nearby through with them. The wheel itself stayed behind in the bakery, inert. Cookie watched it happen and went back to sleep.

## Arrival and the reveal

The six landed in old-growth forest at the foot of an unfamiliar mountain, no roads, no signal, no sign of the modern world. Two hours down a narrow trail with no boot prints, Sable and Aldric ranging ahead as scouts (ghosts, no emitter charge, since none had been brought to the bakery). Two riders approached in Dawnchurch militant kit, crossed-torch-and-bell insignia retired roughly forty years after Sable and Aldric's deaths: the crew was not just elsewhere but elsewhen, at least four hundred years in the past.

The militant branch of the church was active and contemporary in Sable and Aldric's own era; they simply belonged to the archival/support branch and stayed out of militant politics, and the two branches barely interacted. See `world/church-history.md`.

## The central conceit

The two riders are alternate versions of Sable and Aldric, from a timeline where the Hunger was never released. With no Hunger and no collapse, the eastern chapterhouse never fell; it grew into a militant fortress that trains frontline undead-hunters. No archive. No Pell. Full profiles at `the-captain.md` and `the-ritualist.md`.

A bell-ring test confirmed the ghosts weren't mimicry: coherent undead holding shape under resonance, something neither alternate had a category for. The two pairs spent an evening at camp exchanging histories. The ghosts' actual death (Maren, the Hunger, the sacrifice, twenty years trapped) meant nothing to the alternates, who had no Maren, no Pell, no Tem, no crypt in their record, confirming the timelines diverged completely rather than in outcome only. Small, uncanny points of overlap surfaced anyway: same porridge, same crooked fig tree outside the east wall, a nephew's inherited features.

Rook and Mara privately recognized the captain/ritualist dynamic as their own hunter "cleansing-focus" burnout pattern: talented, doctrine-bound people who've stopped protecting anything of themselves and will very likely die young on an assignment nobody pulls them off of. Agnes and Leo felt the uncanny wrongness of being treated as strangers by faces that knew them intimately in their own world. Sable and Aldric each separately concluded the difference between themselves and their alternates was never character, only which hand found them first: Pell's, versus a drill sergeant's.

## The lich hunt

The captain's unit was already hunting a lich that had taken seventeen souls from a nearby town over three months. The ritualist's notebook research connected the wheel's design to the lich's ritual: one half of a matched escape-hatch pair, meaning a second, completed wheel existed somewhere in the mountains, and the crew's own trip through the bakery's wheel may have been what let the lich's half finally register a working destination.

The group was jumped mid-climb by thralls dropping from the canopy; ghosts scouted above bell-resonance range and called flanks, captain/ritualist and Rook/Mara fighting in parallel, uncannily well-coordinated on a single day's acquaintance. No injuries. At the peak, the ritualist's bells went dead, later understood as the lair's entrance being sealed against sound rather than warded against detection. Sable and Aldric felt a homing pull only undead could sense and phased underground, decoding a sealed glyph-door that required a voice with no living pulse to open. A stairway opened from solid rock for the rest of the group: first proof the ghosts' nature was a tactical asset the fortress-trained pair had no equivalent for.

Wraiths tore loose from the passage walls in the dark, a harder fight than the thralls; wraiths don't stagger the same way, low visibility made friendly fire on the ghosts a real risk, and the group fought almost entirely on the ghosts' called warnings. No losses.

The lich was found on a rock platform suspended over a bottomless void, mid-transcription on a second, matching wheel, surrounded by empty jars that had held harvested souls. He recognized the ghosts as proof his own wheel worked, delighted rather than alarmed. Necrotic-energy runework lit the platform and fed his regeneration; the ritualist's bells could stagger him, but wounds sealed almost instantly. He identified the ritualist as the fight's linchpin, pinned in place holding the field and guarded only by the captain's speed, and baited an opening by charging Leo and Agnes at the runework junction, drawing the ritualist's attention away just long enough for a small thrall to land a necrotic injection directly into him. See `the-ritualist.md` for what that did and what followed.

Leo and Agnes broke for the worktable under Rook and Mara's cover, and with the ghosts dictating the glyph sequence in real time, marked a destination onto the second wheel, turning it from a blank "nearest safe alternative" default into a working, targeted door home. The lich, realizing what was happening, abandoned the fight and charged the table. The captain grappled him bodily and dragged him over the platform's edge into the void, taking a mortal wound to the side in the process. Both fell. Sable and Aldric said the old church rites over the ritualist's body, then descended into the void to say the same rites over the captain and the lich's remains, before the crew activated the completed wheel and returned to the bakery in the same instant they'd left. No time passed at home.

## What carries out

- **The wheel.** Handed to the hunter network's vault (contact: Delacroix), logged inert, flagged never to be read aloud or translated aloud again. See `world/objects.md` and `world/hunter-network.md`.
- **Rook's private reaction.** He struggled most with the outcome of the six of them, having rooted for the alternates to survive and get "something good," and finding instead that both timelines independently produced the same brutal math (dead or transformed at 24 to 26), just by different doors. He hasn't said this to Mara in so many words, but she knows.
- **Sable and Aldric.** Each privately concluded the gap between themselves and their alternates was a matter of luck, not character. See `characters/sable-and-aldric.md`.

For the alternates themselves, see `the-captain.md` and `the-ritualist.md`. The ritualist's page carries a substantial epilogue after his death in the fight above.
