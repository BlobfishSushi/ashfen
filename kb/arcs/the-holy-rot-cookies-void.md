# The Holy Rot / Cookie's Void

*Tag: `[CORE]`. Depends on: — (no dependency on other post-foundation arcs).*

The canonical reveal of what Cookie actually is. This arc supersedes Ezekiel & Cookie's Nature's claim to that reveal — see that page's demotion note; it's since been shelved entirely, for unrelated reasons (see `archive/index.md` and `archive/ezekiel-and-cookies-nature.md`). A non-canon alternate ending to this arc also exists, where Cookie doesn't let Sable and Aldric go — see `archive/alternate-endings/the-holy-rot-cookies-void.md`.

## Position and dependencies

Any point after the foundation arcs. No hard dependency, though it reads best after [Cookie's True Nature](cookies-true-nature.md), which introduces Cookie without fully explaining her.

## What happened

A secretive Inquisition-style religious sect — the Office of Lesser Rites, called the Censers by the hunters who eventually take them down — deploys a slow-acting holy energy poison, the rot, against Sable and Aldric. It's accumulation-based rather than acute: no single moment of injury, no wound to treat, just a load that keeps building. Nobody in the crew or the hunter network has seen it before, and there's no known cure.

**How it presents.** Like fever — a deliberate echo of what Sable's poisoning looked like from the outside four centuries ago (see [Origin](origin.md)). **How the living register it:** as an absence. Agnes and Leo both notice, before anything else, that the low-level alarm a living body runs around the ghosts — the thing you stop consciously noticing but never actually stop feeling — has gone quiet. Wrongness by subtraction rather than addition.

**Cookie's decision.** She's present and watching throughout the crisis without acting or explaining — the crew doesn't ask her, because it doesn't occur to any of them that she's an option. Then she eats them. She's the sole decider, works on reasoning she never shares, and gives no warning. Agnes witnesses it and believes, straightforwardly, that Cookie has just eaten Sable and Aldric.

**Inside the void.** The liminal wood under too many stars, the cabin, and the doll-maker — Cookie's true form, a secluded woman with long black hair and green cat-eyes. She dotes on the two of them the way she dotes on the dolls: talking to them, keeping them close, remaking them. The rot is stripped out in the remaking. She comes near to keeping them, and lets them go instead, on the grounds that they're still needed out there.

**Return.** Sable and Aldric are spat back out a week later, cured, with no memory of any of it. The cuddly era ends in that same moment — the rough patch is over, so the kindness stops. Cookie goes back to being Cookie without any transition.

**The week itself.** The crew doesn't sit with it. The hunters and Leo run the sect down over those seven days — Mara's network for reach, Leo's casework for the trail — and take them. The sect is finished by the time the ghosts come back out.

**Agnes's week.** She has no reason to believe anything except that the cat ate her friends.

- *Day one — denial.* She keeps expecting them to turn up. Every cold spot, every flicker in the lights. She doesn't tell anyone she's doing it.
- *Day two — bargaining.* She asks Cookie for a sign, anything. Cookie gives her nothing she can read, and instead begins the cuddly era: in her lap, underfoot, sleeping against her, present in a way she has never been before. Cookie is not a cuddly cat. This is a measure taken for a specific purpose.
- *Day three — acceptance.* She gets a proper cry out of herself and comes out the other side of it.
- *Day four onward.* Back on her feet, working the sect case with the rest of them.

Cookie knows they're coming back. The comfort she offers across those days is real and also, from her side, unnecessary — she's consoling Agnes for a loss that isn't one, and doesn't correct her. Whether that's kindness, incapacity, or indifference to the distinction isn't something the crew can determine.

## Aftermath / what carries forward

This is the standing canonical account of what Cookie is — see `characters/cookie.md`. Sable and Aldric carry no memory of the void; nobody has told them what actually happened during the week.
