## 1. The premise

Present day. A bakery in the city, run by a woman who can see the dead, staffed after hours by two ghosts who have been dead four hundred years and are still working. Around them: a private investigator, a small and territorial network of hunters, a medium, a pair of teenage seers, and a cat that is not a cat.

Cases come to the bakery because the bakery has a reputation. Most of them are hauntings, entities, or people. The register is horror by absence rather than spectacle — the thing that should be there and isn't.

## 3. The bakery crew

### Sable & Aldric

**Background.** Scholar-clerics at the Dawnchurch chapterhouse, archival branch, not frontline fighters — Aldric the archivist, Sable a researcher. Partners for four hundred years. Sable died at 26, Aldric at 24, thirteen days apart.

**Personalities.** Aldric — easygoing, lighthearted. Sable — calm and steady, rarely angered and terrifying when she is.

**Relationship.** Platonic for four centuries; the Dream Eater incident (see event log) added a layer of closeness. Reality pulled them back afterward, but the partnership sits deeper now.

**Day to day.** Guide spirits, work cases — invisible scouts, access to unreachable places, historic and lost knowledge — and otherwise sit at their corner table or watch bread bake.

**Sable's past.** Grew up on the streets, nameless. Caught at ten breaking into the chapterhouse kitchen; Pell took her in rather than turning her out. Sixteen years in the chapterhouse mellowed her considerably, but the fight-for-survival instinct never left, however well hidden.

**Aldric's past.** A small shepherding village in the northern hills, unnamed — locals called it only by its position, "up past the ridge." At twelve, a fast-moving pestilence swept the region, sheep first, then the youngest and oldest, and killed his entire immediate family inside two weeks. He was the sole survivor. Clerics performing rites across the dying region found him and brought him south to the chapterhouse, where Pell took him in.

He has always believed the fever was an ordinary illness, and never questioned it — until the Hans Fischer case reveals it as an entity still active in his family's home region, and reveals his family's death and the present-day Fischer crisis to be the same recurring threat. *(See event log — The Aura Chronicle. Aldric does not know his aunt Aura ever existed; she left the village before he was born and nobody ever told him.)*

**Sable's ghost origin.** She died of what she believed was fever, never knowing Maren had poisoned her. Maren then performed the passing rites over her body — the same rites Sable had performed over other people's dead, as a cleric, for years. Those rites had been corrupted by the entity Maren released, and every soul given them went to the Hunger. Sable worked it out from the other side: the afterlife was wrong, the rites were wrong, and she had been handed to the thing nobody would admit was loose, by a ritual she had trusted and administered herself. That is the source of the fury. Three days crawling back out on it. Aldric was the first person she encountered afterward, and ground the anger into a drive to find the truth rather than something that would have consumed her.

**Aldric's ghost origin.** Died at the door at 24, giving himself as the sacrifice in Sable's place (see event log — Origin). Twenty years trapped afterward, with Sable.

**Ghost category — both, with an asterisk.** Sable reads cleanly as category 3, strong emotion. Aldric fits none of the three categories, and nobody has ever pushed on it — the hunters, and Sable and Aldric themselves, default to category 3 and have never had reason to look closer. What actually holds them both coherent is the Hunger's own essence. *(Not known in-world.)*

**Open thread — Hunger residue.** Aldric's twenty years in the Hunger's dimension also left something dormant in him, confirmed exactly once, as the catalyst behind the pocket reality in the Echo State/Spider arc. Separate from the essence that stabilizes them both, though presumably related. Deliberately unresolved — a Chekhov's gun with no fixed answer. *(Rook and Mara's private instinct that the ghosts' strangeness traces back to the Hunger's dimension is, unknown to them, correct on both counts.)*

### Agnes

Baker. Descends from a bakery Sable and Aldric frequented during Sable's cleric days. A sensitive — a triggering event (see event log) sharpened her sight. Sees Sable and Aldric easily, other ghosts with effort. Serves as the ghosts' front and their bridge to the living world, and holds custody of Cookie.

### Leo Castello

Former cop; left the force after digging into a cold case without authorization. Runs private investigations now, with a reputation for solving strange ones. A great detective who brings a cop's instincts to dangerous cases.

Carries a replica banishment bell — unblessed iron, stuns and repels, banishes nothing, exists to buy him seconds to get out of a room. Carrying and ringing it case after case knocked partial sensitivity loose in him: he hears and sees Sable and Aldric, less clearly than Agnes does.

Functions as a hunter under Mara's jurisdiction without formal training, a status acquired by accident during the Marlowe case.

### Rook & Mara

Hunters, frequent partners. Rook brings muscle and initiative, Mara strategy and cohesion. Both terse and gruff. Both partial sensitives — they see and hear Sable and Aldric directly, less clearly than Agnes.

Mara is senior in the city and knows every hunter in it. Both learned about the ghosts and became secret allies; most of the network still doesn't know, since working with what hunters are meant to banish is controversial.

**Mara's background** *(revealed to the crew in The Wishing House; concealed from the network entirely).* Born **Devlet Manoli**, into eleven generations of working witches — land craft, working craft, a good deal of it the kind the network confiscates or burns. She has total recall for ritual structure, can hear a working done wrong from the next room, and has **no magical aptitude whatsoever**. Tested at three, four, six, nine, eleven, and thirteen; nothing, ever. There is a family word for it that doesn't translate — nearest is "cursed," but it means something closer to an unlucky thing that happens to a household. Nobody was cruel about it. Everyone was kind, and she could not stay.

She left at nineteen and went to the network, where the only question asked is whether the job got done, and where knowing is a weapon when nobody else in the room does. She has protected the family since: eleven hunter enquiries deflected in eight years, and **two falsified site reports**. There is something under a stone lid in the family's yard, kept covered by a woodpile that is restacked twice a year and wants doing more often lately. Mara has known about it her whole life and has never said a word. *[Open: what it is. The crew now knows it exists.]*

**Rook's background** *(surfaced in The Wishing House).* Surname **Manchetti**; Rook is his first name. City family, small apartment, parents Terry and Cathy, older brother **Ross**, younger sister **Rosalind**. When Rosalind was twelve she and their father were caught in a category three incident: he was killed, she was blinded and crippled. She had told the family there was something in the hallway; seventeen-year-old Rook told her there was nothing there. Rosalind is faintly sensitive in her own right and always has been.

**After the Echo State/Spider arc.** Both carry an unprovable instinct that whatever sets Sable and Aldric apart from other spirits traces back to Aldric's twenty years in the Hunger's dimension. No precedent exists to confirm it. They don't discuss it outside each other. It has moved their private read of the ghosts' danger ceiling upward without changing day-to-day trust at all.

### Cora Winstead

Co-founded an architecture firm with her best friend of fifteen years, Daniel. A born medium with innate spirit perception, hidden from everyone except Daniel until her case brought it out.

### Amelie & Victor — the twins

Age 14. True Seers: a bloodline ability to see the future, sometimes skipping generations. Their parents don't know. Involuntary visions since seven, covered socially as narcolepsy. Visions show danger — car accidents, fires, heart attacks — and always come true.

Warning people directly never worked, so they document instead: Amelie draws what she sees (visual only), Victor writes what he hears (auditory only). Both catch flickers and whispers outside full visions.

**Seer lore.** Seers were wiped out roughly a century ago for being too dangerous to warlords and to anyone with plans worth protecting; the last confirmed sighting predates that. Discovery means recruitment or elimination, often both in that order.

**Friends.** Sarah and Matt, childhood friends, who have noticed the twins acting differently since the bakery entered the picture.

### Chen

Detective on the force, Leo's friend. The only person both aware of the supernatural and inside the department. Knows Leo works with ghosts, without the details. Large Chinese family; occasionally invites the whole bakery crew, ghosts included, to family events.

### Cookie

A petite black cat with green eyes. Doesn't age. Her shadow doesn't quite match her body. In Agnes's care.

**True nature.** An eldritch entity that spent ten years disguised as a cat, mouth wide enough to swallow a person, containing voids and stars. Hunted Sable and Aldric as prey through the streets and won, before being told to stop.

**The void.** Behind her mouth is a liminal space — a quiet wood under too many stars, centered on a small wooden cabin. Inside it, Cookie's true form is a secluded woman with long black hair and bright green cat-like eyes, who makes soft plush dolls, content and alone, a basket of leftover scones and cat treats beside her. She talks to the dolls and dotes on them.

**Being swallowed is not necessarily fatal.** What the doll-maker does to what she takes in is remake it. Sable and Aldric were swallowed and remade to strip the holy rot out of them, kept a week, and released — she came near to keeping them and let them go because they were still needed out there (see event log: The Holy Rot / Cookie's Void). Neither remembers any of it.

**Mr. Patterson.** For reasons she has never disclosed, Cookie was an injured kitten on an ordinary patch of grass when he found her and took her in. Ten years followed. He is the first person Cookie ever bonded with, and she keeps his wishes — no eating people — out of love for him, not obligation or fear. It is the only known constraint on her, it is voluntary, and it does not extend to anyone else.

## 4. World rules

### Perception tiers

1. **Born mediums** (Cora) — see all spirits easily.
2. **Sensitives** (Agnes) — a triggering event sharpened her sight; sees Sable and Aldric easily, other ghosts with effort.
3. **Partial sensitives** (the twins, Leo, Rook, Mara) — flickers and whispers, or exposure-induced: Leo via the bell's resonance, Rook and Mara via years in the work. They see and hear Sable and Aldric directly, less clearly than Agnes. *[Open: what specifically made Rook and Mara sensitive — the job in general, hunter training, or a particular incident.]*
4. **Non-sensitives** (most people, Chen) — rely on charms or known tells like cold spots.

### Ghosts — formation

Generally one of three triggers:

1. **Supernatural ability in life.** (E.g. the twins, in the branch where their vision-deaths occur.)
2. **Strong unfinished business.**
3. **Strong associated emotion** — anger or sadness. The most common and most dangerous category, and the basis for the old doctrine that all ghosts are dangerous.

### Ghosts — capabilities

**Baseline, default state, no tech or magic involved:** can't touch objects. With effort: turn visible, chill the air, interfere with lights and electronics. They pass through walls and can reach places the living can't.

**Solid form** *(arc-conditional — requires the emitter)*: solid, tangible, able to interact with the physical world for a few hours per charge. A conditional exception, not a replacement. In any timeline without the emitter arc, baseline is the only mode.

*[Open: the fine grain of the baseline — reading, page-turning, and the archive work they demonstrably do. Also open: range. They travel to the northern hills and through a time-gate without difficulty, so no tether has ever been established.]*

### Ghosts — what harms them

*(Assembled from arcs rather than ever stated as a rule. Check against the event log before treating any line as fixed.)*

- **Salt.** A salt round injured both of them badly enough to force a retreat (the Bakery/Agnes arc).
- **Ward mixtures.** Salt, holy water, and iron shavings — Greta's family ward would have hurt them.
- **The true bell.** Dangerous to them, not only to what it's aimed at.
- **Holy energy.** The rot: accumulation-based, presents as fever, no known cure (see event log: The Holy Rot).
- **Blood loss in solid form.** Blood is spirit energy made liquid; massive loss means energy depletion, and can drop them into an echo state — reliving their deaths on loop — until the bond fades or a passive charge sustains them.

### The hunter network

Modern hunters are what's left after the Dawnchurch's militant branch stopped existing as an institution — remnants of old guilds plus independents who never belonged to one. It works less like an order than like mercenary work.

**Recruitment.** Young, and almost always someone who lost or nearly lost somebody to the supernatural and came looking for answers. Nobody is born into it.

**Structure.** Small and close to flat. A senior hunter oversees a city — Mara has this one, and the roster is not large — assigning jobs and collecting intel. She has no visibility into what hunters in the next city are doing unless they make contact. Clusters don't agree on doctrine, don't share everything, and are under no obligation to.

**The back lines.** The structural part, and the reason a network exists at all rather than a scatter of freelancers: archives, artifact containment, ritual studies, training. They hold the resources, move information between clusters, and get called in when a job turns difficult or strange. *[Open: named back-line contacts and their locations. Delacroix, the vault contact from the Lich's Wheel arc, is the only one so far.]*

**Where the crew sits.** Mara senior in the city, Rook working cases with her, Leo operating under her jurisdiction without training. Most of the network doesn't know about the ghosts and wouldn't approve.

### The church, then and now

The **Dawnchurch** ran chapterhouses, and had at least two branches that barely interacted: **archival/support** (Sable, Aldric, Pell) and **militant** (frontline, bell-trained, crossed-torch-and-bell insignia, retired roughly forty years after Sable and Aldric's deaths). Every cleric was bell-trained regardless of branch.

The chapterhouse of the Origin arc was struck from the records and closed a month after the ritual; its clerics were scattered deliberately. *[Open: what the Dawnchurch is now, if anything, in the present day — and whether the Office of Lesser Rites descends from it.]*

## 5. Objects and devices

### The banishment bell

An old technique of the Dawnchurch's militant branch. Requires iron of a specific design, tuned to a precise resonance, plus the skill to ring it correctly — knowledge lost to time. Every cleric was trained on the bells regardless of branch; if the chapterhouse came under attack it was all hands, archivists included. Sable and Aldric can both ring one correctly, if not to a ritualist's standard.

**The true bell.** The only one known to exist. Stolen from a museum during Leo's first case (see event log) and hidden at the bakery ever since. It genuinely banishes. It is heavy — a two-handed, planted-feet proposition, not something anyone carries — and it is dangerous to Sable and Aldric as well as to whatever it's aimed at. It comes out only when the crew means to banish something and the something warrants it.

**Leo's replica.** Unblessed iron, right shape, wrong resonance. Stuns and repels, banishes nothing. A tool for buying seconds.

**Practical consequence.** The only two people who know how to use the true bell are the two it's most dangerous to, and neither can lift it without the emitter. Everyone else on the crew is working from their instruction rather than training.

### The emitter *(arc-conditional)*

Stephanie Constable's device. Makes ghosts solid, tangible, and visible for a few hours per charge.

**Solid-form tells:** dilated pupils, cold skin, no pulse, breathing that is voluntary and sometimes falls into unnatural rhythm. Dogs know something is wrong; cats don't care.

**Blood.** In solid form, blood is spirit energy made liquid. See *what harms them*, above.

## 7. Alternate-timeline cast — the Lich's Wheel

*Counterparts from a timeline where the Hunger was never released and the eastern chapterhouse became a militant fortress instead of an archive — no Pell, no Maren, no Tem. Both are dead as of the arc's end, in their own world. Kept for callbacks and crossover material.*

**The Captain (alternate Sable).** The same person, diverged completely. Caught at ten stealing bread by a drill sergeant, Voss, who saw a weapon instead of a child, and trained accordingly. Youngest captain in the fortress's history at 24: highest mission success rate, highest squad turnover, nicknamed "the mad dog." Efficient, violent, resigned to dying young, trusts only her ritualist. Died dragging the lich over the edge of the platform into the void, mortally wounded. Buried a week later by a church search team that never learned what happened in that chamber.

**The Ritualist (alternate Aldric).** Same person, same fever-orphan origin, raised at the fortress rather than in an archive. Trained as a ritualist: reads spirit signatures, wields tuned bells for disruption and detection, keeps a blood-stained field notebook of witness testimony. Twelve years partnered with the captain — he herds, she executes — and the only person unafraid of her. Killed by necrotic-energy injection while holding the battlefield alone. The injection also anchored a fragment of him as a masterless thrall for several days, in which state he tracked down Aura using what present-day Aldric had told him, reached her homestead, and died in her arms moments after she recognized him. Buried near her flock; his notebook and bells are all she has left of him.

## 8. Historical cast

*All long dead. Included because their history underpins present-day arcs.*

### The chapterhouse *(Origin arc)*

**Pell.** Senior archivist. Took in a ten-year-old Sable after catching her stealing from the kitchen, and four years later took in Aldric, sole survivor of the regional fever, raising him the same way — gentle, unhurried, no pressure to talk before he was ready. Put both children's sharp minds to work in the archive. One of the two who set up the sealing ritual. Sent south to an archive after the collapse; died there of old age, still trying to preserve a record of what happened.

**Tem.** Close friend of Sable and Aldric, six years younger than Sable, effectively a younger sister to her. Fiercely loyal and willing to act on what she judges important. The other of the two who set up the ritual. Went into hiding rather than take a posting after the collapse, and spent twenty years working to free Sable and Aldric — discovery would have meant being locked up. Died not long after succeeding, her body giving out from the toll of it.

**Maren.** Prior. Released the Hunger by accident, poisoned Sable to keep it quiet, went on performing corrupted passing rites rather than admit either, and sabotaged the sealing ritual to kill everyone who had uncovered it. Exiled afterward to somewhere cold and quiet, without trial or charge, and lived out her days there. *(See event log — Origin, and The Aftermath.)*

**The Hunger.** The ancient entity sealed beneath the chapterhouse, released by Maren, resealed by the ritual that killed Aldric. Its dimension held Sable and Aldric for twenty years. Its essence is what keeps them coherent. *(Not known in-world.)*

### The Aura Chronicle *(~420 years before present)*

**Aura.** Aldric's aunt; youngest of Osric and Agatha's eight children, born a generation behind her oldest sister Marta. A genuine sensitive from early childhood — saw and spoke with minor nature spirits, could coax plants to grow — which marked her a suspected witch in a devout village. Shielded for years by her siblings, she left at sixteen to escape the church's suspicion and to free them of the burden. Settled alone in the northern hills, where she befriended, named, and eventually entered a permanent blood pact with the nature deity she called Lamby, which marked her a warlock in the church's eyes for life. Mother to Nettle. Sole ancestor of every present-day Fischer — Marta's line ended with Aldric. Believed for the rest of her life that her entire birth family died in the fever, and learned only secondhand that a young cleric named Aldric had died in a ritual the church covered up. Died peacefully at 86.

*(An alternate-timeline Aura appears in the Lich's Wheel arc, briefly reunited with an alternate Aldric before his death. That does not touch present-day Aura's canon, in which she never learns her nephew survived at all.)*

**Lamby.** An ancient nature deity, roughly lamb-shaped, that held the same stretch of hills and suppressed the pestilence entity there for longer than any human settlement in the region. Neither good nor evil, and drawn to human company, which it finds interesting. Named by Aura as a joke about mortal audacity, which it was amused enough to keep permanently. Entered the permanent blood pact during the pestilence's escape, protecting Aura and Nettle's land at the cost of diminishing itself permanently into the body of an ordinary lamb. Has stayed with Aura's descendants ever since — present at nearly every birth, wedding, and burial for four hundred years, known to the living Fischers only as "the old lamb," its nature and its name unknown to any of them. Still suppressing the entity in the present day, on a fragile balance.

**The pestilence entity.** Ancient, native to the northern hills, indifferent rather than malevolent — closer to weather than to a monster. Held down by Lamby for centuries, escaped catastrophically once (the fever that killed Aldric's family and most of the region), later re-suppressed and sealed into a cave barrier keyed to Aura's bloodline. Still stirs.

**Nettle.** Aura's daughter. Real if weaker sensitivity from age four; could hear Lamby faintly and befriended it in her own right. Married into a neighboring family at 22; her descendants are every present-day Fischer. Died decades after Aura, of old age.

**Joel.** Nettle's husband, a local man. Knew enough of Aura's history from Nettle to recognize danger when a militant cleric came asking after her, and ran a shortcut to warn the homestead ahead of him.

**Marta.** Aldric's mother, Aura's oldest sister, and effectively her day-to-day parent given the age gap.
