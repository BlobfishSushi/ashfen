# Style files — superseded `[SHELVED]`

Removed 2026-08-29. The `kb/styles/` tree held five files:
`index.md`, `prose-conventions.md`, `authorial-sensibility.md`,
`leo-castello-voice.md`, and `unassigned-bathhouse-voice.md`. This page records
what they were, why they were removed, and where each surviving lesson went, so
nothing has to be relearned by trial and error a second time.

## Why they were removed

Three problems, all producing the same symptom — drafts full of recognizable AI
prose cadence, which is exactly what those files were written to prevent.

**1. They were read at the wrong time.** `prose-conventions.md` was roughly
1,800 words of prohibitions, and `CLAUDE.md` instructed every session to read
it before drafting. A writer agent holding a twenty-item list of things not to
do writes to satisfy the list rather than to write the scene. The writing
skills' own guidance names this failure directly: *"A writer who internalizes
the principle produces natural variation. A writer following an exhaustive
checklist produces something mechanical,"* and its brevity test — *"could a
writer internalize this file in one read? If they need to keep it open as
reference while drafting, it's over-prescribed"* — was failing.

**2. `authorial-sensibility.md` was prescribing the tics.** It distilled the
author's humor mechanics from a first-person comic nonfiction essay and told
writer agents to reproduce them across all characters and arcs: escalation
through parallel clauses, rule-of-three comedic repetition, register-shifting,
long-setup/short-fragment-verdict rhythm. In first-person comic nonfiction
those are the author's real instincts and they work. In third-person fiction
they are within a hair of the default LLM cadence, and an agent instructed to
produce them produces them constantly. The chapter 6 draft shows the result —
see the "escalating triads and fragment-verdicts" entry in
`prose-tics-superseded.md`.

**3. The two voice files documented voices that had never been decided.**
`leo-castello-voice.md` was extrapolated from a single pilot scene;
`unassigned-bathhouse-voice.md` from about ten lines with no character attached.
Both were written in the register of settled fact — POV, tense, comp titles,
signature moves — and then read by writer agents as canon. Neither was.
Sample-of-one extrapolation dressed as a decision is the failure mode to watch
for if a new voice file is ever proposed.

The deeper structural problem behind all three: those files sat in the kb, which
reads as project-specific authority, and so they displaced the skills' craft
layer rather than sitting on top of it. `CLAUDE.md` now sets that precedence
explicitly.

## What was dropped outright, and why

Each of these was a real ruling, and each is already covered — usually better,
and always at the right moment — by the writing skills the project runs on.
Restating them in the kb bought nothing and cost the writer's attention.

| Old rule | Already covered by |
|---|---|
| No em dashes as a rhythm crutch | `/writing-principles` § Punctuation Tells, near-verbatim |
| No vague hedges ("somehow," "in a way he couldn't name") | `/writing-principles` § Trust the Reader; failure-modes § Labeling emotions |
| No unearned omniscient detail the POV character can't know | `/writing-principles` § Transportation — the character's knowledge state |
| Don't re-explain a joke or subtext the reader already got | failure-modes § Adding emotional commentary |
| Don't stack buttons / resolve a scene's tension on its last line | failure-modes § Over-elaborating scope; § Resolving tension prematurely |
| Every character needs distinct rhythm and vocabulary; tags-removed test | failure-modes § Homogenizing character simulations |
| Real speech isn't uniformly composed | failure-modes § Homogenizing character simulations |
| Balanced "I'd rather X than Y" constructions | `/llm-writing` § What to Delete — pairing clauses where one half carries the meaning |

The project-specific *instances* of several of these — the actual lines cut in
editing, the chapter 5 finding — were kept in `prose-tics-superseded.md`, because
a worked example from this manuscript is calibration the generic rule can't
give. The generic rule itself was not restated.
