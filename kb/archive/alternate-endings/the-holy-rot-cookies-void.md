# The Holy Rot / Cookie's Void — Alternate Ending

*Tag: `[ALT-ENDING]`. Diverges from [The Holy Rot / Cookie's Void](../../arcs/the-holy-rot-cookies-void.md) at the remaking, inside the void.*

Everything up through Cookie swallowing Sable and Aldric to strip the rot's holy-energy poison out of them is identical to canon — same sect, same accumulation-based poison, same silent decision made without warning or explanation. The split happens at the line canon draws around what she does next: canonically, "she comes near to keeping them, and lets them go instead, on the grounds that they're still needed out there." Here, she doesn't let go.

## What's different

The remaking goes further than a cure. What Cookie hands back, a week later, isn't two cured ghosts restored to the corner table — it's two ordinary house cats. One dark-furred with sharp green eyes and a temper that shows itself if you get too close to its food. The other lankier, softer-eyed, more patient with strangers than the first. Neither answers to a name yet, because neither has one.

- **No memory.** Nothing of four hundred years, the chapterhouse, the Hunger, or each other as anything but the cat they've apparently always known — littermates, by Cookie's account, though nobody can confirm that from her and she doesn't offer more.
- **No way back.** Not "not yet." When the question finally gets asked outright, Cookie's answer is that there's nothing left underneath to reach — the remaking that would have restored them is specifically the one she chose not to do. Whatever "Sable" and "Aldric" were, as distinct from the two ghosts' raw stabilized essence, didn't survive the trip in this version.
- **Small, unprovable resonances.** One cat won't leave the flour sacks and the cool stretch of stone by the back step. The other spends most of its day at the front window, watching the street like it's expecting someone. Whether that's memory, instinct, or nothing at all is exactly the kind of question the rot itself never got answered, and Cookie won't be the one to answer it now.

## Agnes's reckoning

Canon gives Agnes a week of grief over an assumed loss, resolved when her friends come back exactly as they left. This version gives her the same week — denial, the uncharacteristic cuddly era she reads (correctly, as it turns out) as a kindness she can't interpret, the real cry, back on her feet — and then hands her an answer that isn't the relief she spent the week hoping for.

- She takes the cats in without a second thought. There was never a version of this where she doesn't.
- The hard part isn't losing Sable and Aldric — she already did that grieving, same as canon, before she knew what shape it would take. The hard part is what comes after: two small, warm, undeniably living things that are almost them, in a house built around their absence, and having to hold both truths at once without either one flattening the other. These are not Sable and Aldric. She loves them anyway, immediately, the plain uncomplicated way anyone loves a cat.
- She doesn't tell Leo, Rook, or Mara the full shape of it. "The ghosts moved on" does the job and is close enough to true that it doesn't feel like a lie in her mouth. Cora, given what she can actually see, is the one who eventually asks the real question outright — the only member of the crew nobody can talk around.
- Cookie offers nothing further, and isn't asked to. Whether turning them loose into something simpler than themselves was a mercy, a kindness she genuinely believes in, or just what she felt like doing that week, isn't a distinction the crew — or this page — can settle. That's consistent with how canon leaves the same question about her hanging.

## Why this is filed as an alternate ending, not canon

Every later arc on the board assumes Sable and Aldric exist as themselves, ghosts, at the bakery. This ending removes that permanently and offers nothing to build forward on, so it can't sit inside the active timeline alongside anything written after it — it can only exist as its own closed branch.
