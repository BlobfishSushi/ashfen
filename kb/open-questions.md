## Voice

The prose baseline is third person, past tense, per Leo's pilots. Two voices are settled to a working level (Leo, Agnes) and the rest of the cast has none written. Nothing gets a voice file off one sample — that is what produced the removed `kb/styles/` tree; see `archive/style-files-superseded.md`.

- **[Open: Leo frightened, and Leo failing.]** His voice is settled for two modes, on-the-job guard and off-duty warmth, across two pilots. What it does under real fear, and what it does when the persistence runs out and the thing stays unsolved, is untested. Don't extrapolate it from the pilots. See `characters/leo-castello.md`.

- **[Open: the mentor.]** Leo's ten peaceful years on the force ran under a Somerset figure who shaped the voice and is deliberately offstage: not named, not on the page, no kb entry. Author's call whether he ever becomes a character.

- **[Open: which character and arc the bathhouse fragment belongs to, if any.]** A short fictional fragment exists — roughly ten lines — of a man in a steam-filled bathhouse or prison-like space after three years of isolation, about to "return." Stark and mythic: "white nothingness," "a desert of empty days," "the well of life." The author flagged **Disco Elysium** as the influence, but its solemn interior mode rather than the sardonic catalog-voice. It could be an existing character in a different mode, or a new character entirely. Ten lines is too short a sample to tell an intentional voice signature from an artifact of the passage's brevity. If it gets assigned, the voice notes belong on that character's page, written from a real sample.

- **[Open: everyone else.]** Sable, Aldric, Rook, Mara, Cora and the rest have no voice notes at all. They get them the way Leo got his, from pilots written on purpose, or not at all.
