## [CORE] Origin — Sable & Aldric

Prior Maren accidentally released the Hunger, an ancient entity sealed beneath the chapterhouse, and poisoned Sable to keep her from exposing it. She died believing it was fever.

**What Maren was actually covering.** Two things, and they were inseparable. The release itself — and the fact that the chapterhouse's passing rites had been corrupted by it, so that every soul given those rites was being delivered to the Hunger rather than anywhere else. Admitting the second meant admitting the first. So Maren said nothing and went on performing them, including over Sable's body.

**Day 0.** Sable dies. Maren speaks the rites over her; the rites send her to the Hunger.

**Days 0–3.** On the other side, Sable recognises that the afterlife is wrong, that the rites are wrong, and that the rites she herself had performed over other people's dead had been doing this to them. She crawls back out on that. Three days. Aldric is the first person she encounters.

**Days 3–13.** Sable, Aldric, Tem, and head archivist Pell uncover the conspiracy and prepare a ritual to seal the Hunger back behind its door. The ritual requires a sacrifice. Sable — already dead, and the only one of them who is — is the intended sacrifice. Tem and Pell set the ritual up.

**Day 13.** Maren sabotages it. The intent is to kill everyone who has uncovered the conspiracy — Pell, Tem, and Aldric — and report the whole thing as a ritual accident. The sabotage invalidates Sable's sacrifice; the door will not take her, and it will not close. Aldric gives himself in her place as a last-minute patch and dies at the door at 24. Sable is pulled through with him regardless. Pell and Tem survive; Maren's plan fails in every respect except the two people it wasn't aimed at.

Sable and Aldric spend twenty years trapped in the Hunger's dimension before Tem gets them out — the foundation of their present-day bond.

### The Aftermath — the chapterhouse's collapse

The church chose to bury all of it. A prior sabotaging a sealing ritual and directly causing the deaths of two young clerics was not a scandal it was willing to survive, so the chapterhouse was struck from the records and its doors closed roughly a month after the incident. The clerics were scattered across other houses, deliberately separated.

- **Maren** was exiled to somewhere cold and quiet and lived out the rest of her days there. No trial, no charge, nothing written down.
- **Pell** was sent to an archive in the south and died there of old age.
- **Tem** went into hiding rather than take a posting, and spent twenty years researching a way to get Sable and Aldric out. Discovery would have meant being locked up.
- Pell and Tem managed very little correspondence after the split, but both spent the rest of their lives trying to preserve some record of what actually happened.

*(The outside view of all this is in The Aura Chronicle: the ritual's shockwave felt a hundred miles north, then the chapterhouse closing, the clerics scattering, the church silent on why, and a cover story about a young cleric dead in a ritual accident — which is how the news of Aldric eventually reaches his aunt.)*

## [CORE] The Bakery / Agnes

Sable and Aldric were drawn to the bakery because its bread recipe resembled something from their own era. Agnes was present when Rook shot the ghosts with salt — already mildly sensitive, the blast unlocked full clarity. Injured, the ghosts fled to a warehouse; Agnes tracked them down and offered bread. Rook traced her there; Agnes talked him down, and he agreed to let the ghosts go and call off future hunts on them. Agnes invited the ghosts to haunt the bakery. Over time, solving problems together, she became their front and bridge to the living world.

## [CORE] Leo Castello — Introduction

Leo, having heard rumors the bakery could help solve hard problems, approached Agnes about a cold case he'd left the force over. It turned out to be supernatural and still active. Agnes revealed the ghosts to him; stopping the entity required stealing the banishment bell from a museum. They pulled off the heist together, dealt with the entity, and solved the case. Leo became a bakery fixture. Frequent use of a replica bell (made necessary by his growing caseload) knocked something loose in him — partial sensitivity to Sable and Aldric.

## [CORE] Museum Theft Aftermath

The bell has stayed hidden at the bakery ever since; Leo carries a replica in the field (see world reference — The Banishment Bell). The bell's disappearance became a famous unsolved heist — the thief seemingly bypassed all security with foreknowledge of guard routes. Investigation yielded nothing; the bell never resurfaced publicly.

## [CORE] The Marlowe Case / The Haunted Apartment

*Ordering note: this happens some time after Leo became a bakery fixture. At this point the working crew is just Leo, Agnes, and the ghosts — Cora, the twins, and the wider circle haven't joined yet.*

A haunted apartment building. The building's superintendent, Marlowe, gets caught up in the haunting and is killed by the entity in the process. His ghost tracks down the bakery for help.

The case turns out to be beyond what the crew can solve on their own. Taking a leap, Agnes and Leo go to the hunters' bar to reach out to Rook — who'd left himself as a contact back when he called off the hunt after the salt shot. This is where Mara is first introduced: at the bar, she catches sight of the banishment bell Leo has hidden under his jacket.

The hunters help remove the entity from the apartment; Marlowe is able to move on. In the process, Leo effectively becomes a hunter under Mara's jurisdiction — some tension follows, but once Rook and Mara learn about the ghosts and choose to cover for them, they become permanent allies.

## [CORE] Cora Winstead — The Frame-Up

Cora's business partner Daniel was murdered by his mentee Peter after Daniel discovered embezzlement. Daniel's ghost warned Cora and named Peter as the frame-up architect. Cora was arrested three days later. A lawyer friend connected her to Leo. In the interview cell, Leo brought Sable and Aldric; Cora's attempt to fake not seeing them failed, and she came clean about being a medium. Leo took the case. Over one week, the ghosts monitored Peter, gathered evidence, and protected witnesses.

**Chen's Discovery:** during the investigation, a car chase after Peter (the suspect) let Chen — a detective on the force, Leo's friend — see Leo impossibly know Peter's route: Sable was chasing the car, Aldric shouting directions from above. Chen figured out something supernatural was going on; Leo confirmed he works with ghosts, without giving full details. Chen became a trusted contact — the only person both aware of the supernatural and on the force.

Peter was arrested; Cora was freed.

## [CORE] The Twins — Introduction

The twins' worst vision yet: the entire school dropping dead simultaneously, themselves included. In the vision-death, they woke as ghosts (Amelie blind, Victor deaf in that state) and found Sable and Aldric guiding the dead at the school. Sable offered to help them move on or come to the bakery. They woke in the present with the vision's memory; Amelie painted the ghosts, Victor transcribed talk of "a bakery." They tracked down the bakery and convinced Leo their visions were real, since Amelie had painted Sable and Aldric without ever meeting them.

## [CORE] The Marcus Webb Case

Marcus Webb, a groundskeeper and former hunter, plotted to steal the school's life force for himself. The twins had visions of Marcus catching and killing them mid-investigation, letting them avoid that fate — revealing that visions reflect events that *did* happen in another timeline, sent back for a chance to prevent them. The bakery crew and hunter network took Marcus down together. Rook identified the twins afterward as true Seers.

## [CORE] Cookie's True Nature

Mr. Patterson, who'd taken in Cookie ten years prior — an injured kitten on an ordinary patch of grass, for reasons she has never disclosed to anyone — died peacefully of old age but didn't move on — unwilling to let Cookie end up in a shelter. His ghost (with Cookie following) reached the bakery; he asked Agnes to care for Cookie and find her a home, and Agnes agreed. That night, a hungry Cookie revealed her true nature as an eldritch entity that had spent ten years disguised as a cat, hunting Sable and Aldric as prey through the streets and winning. Mr. Patterson's ghost caught her and gave her a stern talking-to: no eating people. She hasn't chased them since. He is the first person Cookie ever bonded with, and she keeps the rule out of love for him — the only constraint on her, voluntary, and not transferable to anyone else.

## [CORE] The Dream Eater

The crew was trapped in a sophisticated illusion. Inside it, Sable and Aldric built a life together and fell in love. Rook disrupted the illusion on their engagement night, before it went further. Aftermath: shifted Sable and Aldric's relationship from purely platonic to something deeper, though reality pulled them back from where the illusion had taken them.

## [BACKGROUND] The Aura Chronicle — ~420 years before present

*This is deep background, not an active present-day arc — it happened roughly four centuries before the present day, beginning a generation before Aldric's own childhood, and its events are complete and unchangeable. It's included because it's the origin of the Fischer family, the deity later called "Lamby," and the pestilence entity central to the Pestilence/Lamb arc. Treat this section as settled canon underlying that arc; draw on it for detail, texture, and things Aldric might eventually learn, rather than as something to be replayed or altered.*

**Family:** Aura is the youngest of eight children born to Osric and Agatha, shepherds in an unnamed village in the northern hills (referred to locally only by its position — "up past the ridge" — not by any name of its own; this is the same village that, generations later, raises Aldric). Birth order: **Marta** (oldest daughter), then six brothers — **Wendel, Bram, Corwin, Halvard, Tobin, Garrick** — then Aura, born well after the others, closest in practice to Marta, who effectively raised her.

**Childhood — the sensitivity:** Aura shows real, quiet sensitivity from a young age — she sees and speaks with minor nature spirits invisible to everyone else, and can coax plants to grow unnaturally fast. The village, devout and insular, first reads it as an odd child's imagination; suspicion hardens as other children notice. Marta and the six brothers — Marta 15, Aura 6, at the point they first organize — form a standing, unspoken pact to shield her: covering for her in the village, keeping her out of sight at the right moments, managing what the parents and the church are allowed to see. They do this understanding their parents would likely prioritize the family's standing with the village over one child among eight, and choosing to protect Aura regardless.

**Father Ansel's suspicion:** The village priest, Ansel, grows suspicious over Aura's childhood, eventually reporting to the Dawnchurch chapterhouse. At twelve, Aura is brought to the chapterhouse for evaluation (to rule out possession) and refuses to enter — screaming that something under the building is awake and starving. The clerics can't get her inside; she's sent home. The chapterhouse itself is later confirmed (via the Origin arc) to have the Hunger sealed beneath it — Aura's instinct was accurate. The incident deepens Ansel's suspicion rather than resolving it.

**The facade (ages ~12–16):** The siblings actively coach Aura in passing as ordinary — what to say, how to move through the village unremarked, which names never to speak aloud. It holds for several years at real cost to all of them.

**Departure:** At sixteen, Aura leaves before dawn, telling only Marta, partly because she's done hiding and partly to free her siblings from years of covering for a "witch's" family. Marta is pregnant at the time (this child does not survive; a later letter delivers the news). The siblings maintain the fiction to the village that Aura ran away; they secretly keep in touch and send supplies via traveling peddlers and drovers, primarily through Bram.

**Exile and the deity:** Guided by minor spirits, Aura settles in a remote clearing in the hills and lives off the land. She decides consciously to lean into what the village already called her, rather than keep shrinking herself. On a mushroom-gathering trip she witnesses a being — old, roughly lamb-shaped, neither good nor evil — driving back something wrong and sick that had surfaced nearby. She returns to the site repeatedly over following weeks; the being warms to her. Through trial and error she attunes enough to catch fragments of its meaning. It reveals (in pieces, over years) that it has held this land, and pushed the same lingering sickness back into the ground, alone, for longer than the village has existed. It's drawn to human company — finds people "interesting," approves of what shepherds do for a flock, since Aura is one herself. She names it **Lamby**, which amuses it enough (the audacity of a mortal naming something older than the church) that it accepts the name permanently. Over years she learns real nature-magic from it: true names, intent, workings that hold.

**Joran:** Some years into her exile, a lost hunter named Joran stumbles onto her homestead, injured. They fall into a relationship that lasts a season. He never learns the extent of what she is until he witnesses a small working done without thinking; he leaves without a word the next morning and is never seen again — he doesn't appear to have reported her, but she never learns why, or where he went. Aura discovers she's pregnant after he's already untraceable.

**Marta's visit / Nettle's birth:** Aura writes Marta about the pregnancy, asking her not to come. Marta comes anyway, leaving her two-year-old son (Aldric) in the care of Osric and her brothers, and stays through the birth and a fortnight after — the two sisters trade eight years of unshared history in that stretch. Together they name the daughter **Nettle**, after a grandmother neither sister remembers well enough for the name to carry any other weight. Marta returns home; her promise to eventually bring Aldric to meet his aunt is never fulfilled — the church's grip on the region tightens over the following years, making travel too risky.

**The next several years:** Nettle, at four, shows real (if weaker than Aura's) sensitivity — she can hear Lamby directly, if faintly, and befriends it in her own right. Osric dies of old age when Nettle is six; Agatha follows the next spring. Church suspicion of "witchcraft" resurges regionally around this time (unrelated at first to Aura specifically), and letters between Aura and Marta thin to almost nothing as the risk of correspondence grows. Marta inherits Osric's flock, managed with her husband and young Aldric's increasing help.

**The Fever (the pestilence's escape):** Roughly when Nettle is ten (Aldric, two years older, is twelve), Lamby comes to Aura and Nettle with an urgency it's never shown before: the entity it has suppressed for longer than living memory — provoked loose by an unrelated, unrecorded working attempted by someone in a neighboring village — has broken free and will sweep the region, killing indiscriminately. Lamby can protect Aura and Nettle and their land specifically, but only via a permanent blood pact, which will (a) mark Aura unmistakably as a warlock to any trained cleric, putting her on the church's kill list for life, and (b) force Lamby to diminish itself permanently into the physical shape of an ordinary lamb, in order to share enough of itself to ward the land. Aura accepts. The pact is sealed at the hollow where they first met — Aura ties ribbons and bells into Lamby's horns, speaks bound words fed to her syllable by syllable, and opens a cut across her palm that scars permanently. Lamby cannot extend protection any further than Aura's own land; it stays with her and Nettle among the flock, unable to reach anyone else, including Aura's family.

**The village side:** The fever hits lambs first, then the youngest and oldest villagers, spreading fast once it takes hold. Doctors and church blessings alike do nothing. Ansel is among the first adults to die. Aldric's cousins die in twos and threes across two weeks; Marta and her husband fall at the end of the second week, Marta using her last strength to tell Aldric — twelve years old, the sole survivor — that he has an aunt somewhere north and west, without details, before she dies. Chapterhouse clerics, performing rites across the dying region, find him the only living thing left in the village and take him south to the chapterhouse — the same one Aura once refused to enter. Pell, head archivist (having taken in another orphan, a feral young thief named Sable, four years prior), receives him gently and puts him to work in the archive. Marta's dying wish — that Aldric survive and live long past the fever — is only partially honored by what follows.

**Aura's aftermath:** Behind her wards, Aura senses the fever pass — indifferent, not malevolent, more force of nature than monster. The wards hold; she and Nettle stay well. The silence afterward — no travelers, no birdsong, distant clerics performing mass rites on the far hills — is worse than the fever itself. Weeks later, once it's safe enough to risk the ride, she travels to her home village and finds it empty: doors open, homes stripped and abandoned, a single unmarked mass grave too large to count. No one is left to tell her who's in it. She returns home believing herself the last of her family, and tells Lamby and Nettle the truth plainly rather than softening it. She commits to rebuilding rather than dwelling — recognizing her siblings' resemblance already living on in Nettle, and resolving not to let that die out.

**Rebuilding:** Over the following years, two other, less devoutly religious families settle nearby, easing Aura's isolation. The pestilence stirs occasionally over subsequent years but never again at the first wave's scale — Aura and Lamby together suppress each flare-up. Aura keeps careful, quiet watch on the church's movements to avoid ever being found. Nettle marries into one of the neighboring families at 22; the homestead grows in anticipation of a larger family.

**Learning of Aldric's fate:** Aura and Nettle (and Lamby) feel a powerful shockwave one winter night — the same ritual that seals the Hunger and traps Sable and Aldric together for twenty years (see Origin arc). Aura reinforces her wards, suspecting danger, but nothing follows. Weeks later, rumors reach her secondhand through in-laws: the chapterhouse is closing, clerics scattering, the church silent on why; then that a ritual killed a young cleric, called an accident by the prior; then nothing further, the story cut off too cleanly for an accident. An offhand mention of the dead cleric's name — Aldric, not a common name, chosen specifically as a compromise between her father's and grandfather's names when Marta named her second son — confirms it for her, though she initially grasps for the possibility of coincidence.

She rides to the chapterhouse the next day. She can no longer sense the hungry presence she felt there at twelve — sealed, evidently by the same ritual that produced the shockwave. She can't enter the abandoned building, but questions the frightened local nature spirits, who confirm: a powerful ritual, a young sacrifice with curls like hers, and relief that whatever it sealed is gone. She sits outside the chapterhouse until near sunset, then rides home in silence, telling no one that night beyond what her bearing already made obvious. She never learns the fuller truth — that Aldric survived as a trapped, then ghostly, presence rather than dying outright.

*Note on how this sits with her: she'd have preferred a monster she and Lamby could fight. What she got instead was a closed door, and the regret of having assumed her whole family dead in the mass grave rather than ever confirming it — an assumption that, in hindsight, cost her the chance (however dangerous and unlikely) of ever finding her nephew in time. Lamby's counsel — that any earlier attempt to search or claim him from inside the chapterhouse would very likely have gotten them both killed, given her marked status — is true and doesn't make the grief lighter.*

**Later life and legacy:** Aura raises grandchildren and great-grandchildren on the land, watching her siblings' features (Corwin's chin, Bram's laugh) resurface generation after generation, alongside a slowly thinning sensitivity — full clarity in Nettle, flickers and occasional unexplainable knowing in the generations after. What can't be passed down as lived sensitivity, Aura teaches as rote tradition — workings and wards performed correctly by descendants who no longer feel why they work. Lamby remains permanently among the flock, unremarked as anything but "the old lamb," present at every birth, wedding, and burial without the family ever understanding what it actually is.

*(Bridging note, inferred rather than explicitly dramatized: at some point following the initial crisis, Aura and Lamby appear to have done further work binding the weakened pestilence into a cave barrier keyed specifically to Aura's bloodline — this is what later requires a Fischer descendant, Hans, to open it in the present-day Pestilence arc. The scene of that working was never written; flag if you want it filled in.)*

Aura dies peacefully at 86; Nettle decades later. The **Fischer** surname enters the family line roughly a century after Aura's death (via marriage, no deeper significance) and sticks permanently. By the present day — some 420 years after Aura's exile — the family numbers a few dozen descendants, six of them still on the original land, including **Hans Fischer**. All present-day Fischers, Hans included, descend from Aura and Nettle — **not** from Marta's line, which ended entirely with Aldric. This makes Hans and the other Fischers Aldric's cousins via Aura (Aldric's aunt), not his direct descendants — a lineage correction from earlier notes, which had implied direct descent from Aldric.

No living Fischer remembers Aura by name. Lamby does, and still sees her in them — in a stubborn jaw, an ease with the flock, a rare flicker of a child who looks a beat too long at something no one else can see.

**On Aura and the church:** Her relationship to the church never resolved into a single clean verdict. Raised devout, she never stopped half-missing the sense of community it gave her parents. The turn against it was personal, not ideological — Ansel's suspicion, years of her siblings managing a lie, an institution that made no room for what she was regardless of how good-willed any individual cleric might be. The pact made her formally hunted by it for the rest of her life. And the same institution that drove her out was also the one that took in her orphaned nephew with real gentleness (Pell), and the one that later used him as an unwilling sacrifice and lied about it. She held gratitude and grief toward it simultaneously, without ever needing one to cancel the other out.

### [CORE] The Ashfen Crypt

A construction crew breaks into an underground crypt with two dozen bodies. Dr. Kaia Okonkwo (Cordova State University) takes over the site. ASH-17 and ASH-18 — the last interred, a woman and man buried side by side — are Sable and Aldric's remains. The crypt also holds a small iron-bound door, nailed shut, that won't open.

Leo and Agnes pose as genealogical researchers, feeding Kaia documents (letters from a woman named Edlyn, a traveling bishop's complaints, property records) to build a preservation case. Mort Voss, leading the mill consortium that wants the land, fights the order. A hearing grants six months' protection. The door stays sealed. Kaia eventually learns the truth about the ghosts. The crypt becomes a protected site.

### [CORE] The CT Scans

Kaia's team CT-scans the remains. The X-rays pull Sable and Aldric into their bones — a doubled awareness at first. Technician Derek runs repeat scans troubleshooting corrupted data; each scan reinforces the bond. By the eighth scan the ghosts are trapped in their bones for over a week, unable to move or speak except to each other. Agnes and Leo check on them repeatedly. They're freed when the bond fades.

**Ongoing:** every semester a new grad student scans the bones; the ghosts get pulled in every time. They've learned to endure it and listen in on the students. It's become a minor university legend. The bones stay in the collection as "scientifically significant."

*(Depends on: The Ashfen Crypt arc — the bones must already be excavated and in the university's collection.)*

### [CORE] Yuki, Stephanie, and the Emitter

Yuki Tanaka, a grad student in Kaia's lab, works out the truth fast and confronts Kaia; she joins the circle, helping protect the ghosts during scans.

Yuki sends the corrupted CT data to Stephanie Constable (signal processing engineer, defense contractor). Stephanie finds the interference is structured, not random — builds a filter that decodes it into images of the ghosts, and finds a related frequency that *amplifies* spiritual energy instead of trapping it.

Stephanie builds **the emitter** — makes the ghosts solid, tangible, visible. Tested first in the bakery, then in the city at night, then in daylight. Solid-form tells: dilated pupils, cold skin, no pulse, voluntary breathing that sometimes falls into unnatural rhythms. Dogs sense something's wrong; cats don't care.

**The Trials:** durability tests — pain response, blood loss, injury recovery. Blood turns out to be spirit energy in liquid form; massive blood loss = energy depletion. An amplification frequency makes them fully solid/interactive for a few hours at a time. First use: they eat bread, hold hands, read books. Food falls through them when the charge fades.

*(Depends on: The CT Scans arc — the corrupted scan data is what starts this chain. Also required before it can be used in later arcs — Julian mission, Spider mission, Fischer piano/fair.)*

### [CORE] The Morrison Building / The Serpent

Cora alerts the crew to a medium convention in a building she condemned years ago, haunted by an ancient shadow-serpent that's fed on spirits for decades. The crew infiltrates. Everett Cross, the convention organizer, is a sensitive manipulated by the entity posing as his mentor. The entity is invisible to hunters — only mediums and ghosts perceive it.

Sable is pulled into a séance and bluffs the entity into retreating, claiming the crew is more powerful than they are. Everett is devastated to learn he's been used. Maya Ivanovna, a genuine medium who came hoping to switch off her abilities, joins the circle.

### [CORE] The Julian Mission / The Siren Vampire

A siren-type vampire, Julian, has been killing club-goers at Velvet (owned by vampire Celeste) — victims leave charmed and compliant, later found dead with no signs of foul play. A young hunter (Daniel Vega, pre-turning) goes missing investigating him.

The ghosts go in as bait using solid forms, aiming to draw Julian out and signal Rook and Mara, waiting outside. Julian targets Sable and Aldric — his charm works through voice, eyes, and touch, touch being worst. He kisses them in an alley to reinforce the charm and takes them to his Victorian house. Sable manages to hit the panic button when his attention splits toward Aldric.

**The Violation:** Julian spends hours undressing and touching them, using the charm to make them want it. They forget their own names but hold onto each other throughout. He's fascinated by their bond and tries and fails to break it.

**The Rescue:** hunters arrive 30 minutes after the panic signal, find Julian standing over the still-charmed ghosts with their blood on his lips. Julian is captured.

**Aftermath:** the ghosts don't use the emitter for months, flinch at sudden sounds, stay withdrawn. Agnes and Leo keep vigil. Technically a success; doesn't feel like one.

**Daniel Vega's fate:** turned by Julian; hides in a cave weeks, surviving on deer blood. Rook and Mara find him with the ghosts' help. Celeste takes him in, teaches him to feed safely and control his siren pull. Officially dead in hunter records; unofficially still hunting.

### [CORE] The Echo State / The Spider

The ghosts scout for Rook and Mara, tracking a giant spider taking hikers. It ambushes the hunters, webbing them toward its nest. The ghosts solidify, shield Rook and Mara with their own bodies, and take multiple spikes of chitin/silk through chest, side, thigh, shoulder while dragging them clear. Massive blood loss (spirit energy) drains them into **echo states** — reliving their deaths on loop — before they collapse.
